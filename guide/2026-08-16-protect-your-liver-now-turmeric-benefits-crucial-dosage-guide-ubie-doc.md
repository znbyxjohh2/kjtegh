Explanation

Turmeric, particularly its active component curcumin, has been studied for its potential benefits on liver health. Research indicates that curcumin may help in reducing liver enzymes, which are markers of liver damage, and improve liver function. However, studies also suggest that turmeric can result in liver damage in certain patients.

Liver Enzymes: One study found that curcumin can lower liver enzymes in patients, suggesting it may help protect the liver.

Non-alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (NAFLD): Another study demonstrated that curcumin might be beneficial in treating NAFLD by reducing liver fat and inflammation.

Dosage: The studies often used doses of curcumin ranging from 500 to 1000 mg per day. However, it is important to consult with a healthcare provider before starting any supplement to ensure it is safe and appropriate for individual health needs. As discussed, some studies also suggest turmeric may have a role in causing liver damage and adding turmeric to your regimen should be discussed with your doctor.

Overall, while turmeric shows promise for liver health, more research is needed to fully understand its effects and optimal dosage.
