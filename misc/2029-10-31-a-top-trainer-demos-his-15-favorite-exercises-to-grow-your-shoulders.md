# A Top Trainer Demos His 15 Favorite Exercises to Grow Your Shoulders

If you've always struggled with building up your shoulders, trainer and founder of Built with Science Jeremy Ethier made the difficult muscle group a major point of focus. He's previously shared his insights on how to build wide shoulders, 3 quick tips to build bigger rear delts, and how to master pullups. Now, he's sharing his 15 favorite exercises you should be doing to get the shoulder gains you're lacking.

"How many of you have hit your shoulders with everything in the book, yet are disappointed because they just won't seem to grow very well?" he says. "What you need, and what I wish someone told me back in the day is more structure behind your shoulder training."

Ethier breaks down the best exercises for each of the three regions of your shoulders, shows you how to pick the right ones for your body and equipment availability, and how to combine them into a weekly routine. That's the structure you've been missing.

Front Delts

"Its muscle fibers run almost 45 degrees to the side relative to your torso. This means that to best activate this muscle, you want to avoid the common mistake of flaring your elbows out to the side during overhead pressing," says Ethier. "This is not only a riskier position for your shoulder to be in, but it also fails to line up with the front delt muscle fibers."

Instead, he explains you should move your elbows into the scapular plane, which is slightly in front of the body (think elbows 45 degrees in relation to your torso, just like he mentions above).

Exercise 1: Standing Barbell Overhead Press

Use a narrower grip that allows you to position your elbows directly under the bar and slightly in front of your torso. Squeeze your glutes, brace your core and push directly overhead while keeping your lower back neutral.

Use a heavier weight, and aim for sets of 6 to 12 reps.

Exercise 2: Standing Dumbbell Overhead Press

Ethier cites a 2013 paper that found that this overhead exercise had the highest activation across all three heads of the shoulder compared to three other pressing variations. However, it can get increasingly difficult to overload once you get to heavier weights. For this variation, choose a lighter weight and aim for a much higher total of reps per set (he suggests 20 to 30, but you should aim even lower if you're just starting out). This way you can still take your shoulder muscles close to failure without letting your setup be the limiting factor.

Exercise 3: Seated Dumbbell Overhead Press

The benefit of a seated press is that the bench provides more stability as you isolate the shoulders. For these, press in the scapular plane and keep your elbows stacked directly under the dumbbells.

Exercise 4: Kneeling Landmine Press

This move will help you work on any muscle imbalances you may have, especially if you usually only press with a barbell. Since you're pressing the weight with one arm, Ethier notes that your core will have to work hard to prevent your torso from rotating too far to the loaded side. This can translate to better strength in compound movements like the deadlift and squat. As you perform the landmine press, focus on reaching forward rather than just pressing up.

Exercise 5: Bodyweight Pike Pushups

Pike pushups align the elbows in the way the front delt fibers run, according to Ethier. To set it up, bring your feet close to your hands to make an upside down V shape and perform a press. If these are too hard, you can start out by performing them on your knees instead. On the other hand, if you feel that they're too easy, elevate your feet onto a higher platform.

Lateral Head (Side Delts)

"The key to their growth is to use a variety of exercises that stress this area in different ways," Ethier says.

Exercise 1: Dumbbell Lateral Raise

The best way to perform this exercise for the most activation and safety of your shoulder joint is to slightly lean forward, raise your arms in the scapular plane slightly in front of your body, with your thumbs positioned slightly above your pinkies. Check out our guide for some extra tips.

Exercise 2: Lean Away Cable Lateral Raise

Ethier notes that the side delts only start to become highly active once you pass the first third or so of a raise exercise. To make the most of this, hang onto the cable machine with your non-working arm and slightly lean toward the direction of the raise to make sure the delts are working throughout the whole range of motion. If you don't have access to a cable machine, you can also just tie a resistance band to a fixture and use that instead.

Exercise 3: Standing Cable Y Raise

This exercise will provide a different stress to the shoulder by taking your side delts all the way to their fully contracted position. Cross the cables, and then pull the cable up into a wide 'Y' position. This movement will also highly activate your lower traps.

Exercise 4: Chest Supported Y Raise

This exercise can help to replicate the movement above without a cable machine. To perform it, set up a bench to 45 degrees and raise your arms into a wide 'Y' shape holding onto light dumbbells. Think about raising your arms out rather than up.

Exercise 5: Barbell Upright Rows

We usually don't recommend this exercise because of the high risk of injury. If you want to give it a try, think about using dumbbells instead. Ethier also offers tips for a safer row, widening your grip to two times shoulder width. Raise your elbows only up to the level of your shoulders, and at the top position ensure that your hands are not below your elbows, but are rather raised up to same level.

Posterior Head (Rear Delts)

"This part of your shoulder that's key to balancing out the look of your front delts by rounding out the back of the shoulder," says Ethier. "If you take a look at how these muscle fibers run, it's at a roughly 45 degree angle. This means that to best target this muscle, you want to use exercises where your elbows can pull in this 45 degree angle relative to the torso."

Exercise 1: Rear Delt Swing

Grab a pair of light dumbbells and lay on an incline bench set at a 30 degree angle. Then swing your elbows back behind your body while keeping your arms straight and angled out at about a 45 degree angle. Ethier suggests that you push hard and do as many reps as you can. Once you fatigue, immediately drop set to the next exercise.

Exercise 2: Rear Delt Row

Using the same setup and dumbbells, you can take the rear delts to complete failure by turning the swing into a row by simply bending your elbows. With this slight modification, you'll be bale to continue doing more reps until your rear delts are completely fatigued.

Exercise 3: Standing Barbell Rear Delt Row

Use an overhand relatively wider grip that enables you to pull high to the level of your sternum and with your elbows angled out at about 45 degrees. Think about pulling with your elbows as you do so.

Exercise 4: Rear Delt Cable Row

This same move can be applied to a cable machine using a wider attachment and that sweet spot 45 degree angle.

Ethier says this exercise trains the rear delts in its fully stretched position, an important mechanism of muscle growth. Reach your arm to your opposite ear, and then pull your elbow across and then behind your body. You can also do these with a resistance band, too.

Putting It Together

According to Ethier, research shows that the front delts are already highly activated during exercises you already have in your training plan, like the bench press.

"So since your front delts are already likely well developed, you're only going to pick one main front delt exercise to do throughout the week. You'll want to emphasize the side and rear delts more by picking two exercises from each of those categories. Aim for three to four sets of these exercises, and throw them into your existing workouts throughout the week, or do it all in one shoulder workout," says Ethier.
