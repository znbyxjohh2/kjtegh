What are the primary causes of elbow joint pain?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Elbow joint pain is commonly caused by injuries from falls, sports, or repetitive motions such as lifting, typing, or throwing. It can also stem from underlying conditions like arthritis, bursitis, tendonitis (tennis or golfer's elbow), or nerve compression. Symptoms may include swelling, stiffness, weakness, or difficulty straightening the arm.

Because elbow pain has many possible causes—ranging from minor strains to conditions requiring medical care—identifying the source early is key to effective treatment and preventing long-term damage. Explanation

Elbow joint pain can come from many different issues. One common cause is trauma. This can happen when someone falls on their arm or the elbow, leading to a fracture, dislocation, or ligament injury. When the elbow is injured, the bones or soft tissues may be damaged, causing pain. Another cause is repetitive stress. This happens when people use their arm in the same way over and over again. It might cause tendinitis (inflammation in the tendon) such as in tennis elbow or golfer's elbow. These conditions hurt because the tendon becomes inflammed from overuse or improper form. Arthritis is another reason the elbow might hurt. In arthritis, the joint can become swollen and painful over time because of wear and tear. Xrays are useful in evaluating arthritic changes to the joint. If you're experiencing elbow pain and want to better understand what might be causing it, try our free symptom checker to help identify potential causes. By knowing the exact cause of the pain through a careful exam and sometimes imaging tests, doctors can decide the best way to treat the discomfort.
