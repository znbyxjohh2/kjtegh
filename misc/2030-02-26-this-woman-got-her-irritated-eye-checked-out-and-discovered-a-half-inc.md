# This Woman Got Her Irritated Eye Checked Out — and Discovered a Half-Inch Worm

What started out feeling like a minor eye irritation turned into a gnarly 20-day health odyssey for one Oregon woman after she discovered tiny worms in one of her eyes.

Abby Beckley, 26, had discomfort and redness in her left eye in the summer of 2016 while aboard a salmon fishing boat in Alaska. After a week of discomfort, she took a closer look and was stunned to discover a threadlike, clear worm about a half-inch long on her eye.

"When I pulled out that worm," she told The Oregonian, "I was just in shock.”

Local doctors couldn’t help her, so Beckley flew down to Oregon and met with physicians at Oregon Health and Science University in Portland. They sent worm samples to scientists at the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention who eventually identified the parasite.

Called Thelazia gulosa, it’s typically seen in cattle eyes in parts of the U.S. and Canada, according to scientists from the Division of Parasitic Diseases and Malaria at the CDC and colleagues, who wrote up a case study about Beckley’s eyeworms published this week in the American Journal of Tropical Medicine and Hygiene.

It's never been found in humans before, according to the authors.

The parasite is spread by flies that thrive on the tears that lubricate the eye, the authors said, and the scientists believe Beckley may have gotten it by walking through cattle pastures before her trip to Alaska.

A total of 14 worms were removed from her eye over a 20-day period — some by doctors and some by Beckley.

She never took medication or used creams. The doctors flushed her eyes on a few occasions, but only advised removing the worms by hand. She’s been symptom-free, the study authors reported, and no further worms have appeared since that time.
