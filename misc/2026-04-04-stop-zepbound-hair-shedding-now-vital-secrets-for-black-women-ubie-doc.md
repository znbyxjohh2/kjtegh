Why Zepbound Can Lead to Hair Shedding

Rapid Weight Loss:A sudden 10% or more drop in body weight over a few months can shift hair follicles from their growth phase (anagen) into a resting phase (telogen).

Nutrient Gaps:Cutting calories may reduce intake of key nutrients—protein, iron, zinc, biotin—that hair needs.

Hormonal Shifts:GLP-1/GIP agonists can alter appetite hormones, indirectly affecting hair cycle regulators.

Stress Response:Any major change—diet, body image, lifestyle—can trigger a stress response, further contributing to telogen effluvium.

Remember: telogen effluvium is typically reversible once your body adapts and nutrient intake steadies. The goal is to minimize shedding and accelerate regrowth.

Hair Care Practices for Maximum Retention

Your styling routine can either protect or exacerbate shedding. Adopt gentle practices:

Low-Manipulation StylesOpt for loose braids, twists or buns that don't stress the hairline.

Sulfate-Free, Moisturizing CleansersSulfates strip oils; look for gentle cleansers with glycerin or coconut-derived surfactants.

Deep Conditioning WeeklyIngredients like shea butter, avocado oil and hydrolyzed protein replenish moisture and strengthen strands.

Limit Heat & Chemical TreatmentsExcessive blow-drying, straightening and coloring can weaken hair already vulnerable from shedding.

Satin or Silk at NightSwap cotton pillowcases for satin to reduce friction and breakage.
