Diabetes Screening

Diabetes screening is the process of testing for diabetes in individuals at risk of developing the disease or those with early signs and symptoms. Early detection is crucial in managing and preventing diabetes-related complications such as heart disease, kidney disease, and nerve damage.

There are specific criteria for diabetes screening based on age, weight, family history, and other risk factors. The American Diabetes Association recommends screening for prediabetes and diabetes for all individuals aged 35 and above.

There are several types of diabetes screening tests, and the choice of test depends on individual circumstances.

The three most common tests are the Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG) test, the Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT), and the Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) test.

Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG) test: The FPG test measures the amount of glucose in the blood after fasting for eight hours.

Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT): The OGTT measures the amount of glucose in the blood after consuming a sugary drink.

Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) test: The HbA1c test measures the average blood sugar levels over the past two to three months.

Diabetes screening for individuals with a family history of diabetes, those who are overweight or obese, and those with high blood pressure or high cholesterol is especially important. Individuals who have had gestational diabetes during pregnancy are also at an increased risk of developing type 2 diabetes.

The frequency of diabetes screening depends on the individual’s age, risk factors, and test results. Those with normal results should repeat the test every three years, while those with borderline or high results should undergo further testing and frequent monitoring.

Screening is crucial for early detection and prevention of complications related to diabetes. A concierge doctor can provide personalized diabetes screening based on individual circumstances and risk factors.
