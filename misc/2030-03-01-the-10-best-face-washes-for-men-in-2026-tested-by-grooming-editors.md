What to Consider

Key Ingredients

A face wash is the cornerstone of any skincare routine—without it, the rest of your products won’t work as well. Choose one that’s gentle yet effective because even if your entire routine is just washing your face, you don’t want a cleanser that strips away your skin’s natural oils.

Occlusives: Occlusives in cleansers help form a protective film on the skin to reduce water loss, making them especially valuable for dry or sensitive types. Ingredients like shea butter, cocoa butter, or castor oil create a barrier that soothes and repairs while washing, preventing the tightness that some cleansers can leave behind. Shea butter is particularly versatile since it also softens the skin as an emollient. While occlusives are nourishing, they may feel heavy for those with oily or acne-prone skin and can sometimes contribute to clogged pores.

Humectants: Humectants are hydration boosters that draw water into the skin, which is critical when cleansing can strip away natural moisture. In face washes, glycerin and hyaluronic acid are standout humectants that keep skin plump and comfortable. Hyaluronic acid, in particular, is ideal for dry skin cleansers, especially when paired with barrier-repairing ceramides and nourishing plant oils like avocado, jojoba, or sunflower seed oil. Together, these ingredients replenish hydration while cleansing, leaving skin supple instead of stripped.

Emollients: Emollients smooth and soften the outer skin layer, making cleansers feel more gentle and restoring flexibility to rough patches. Examples include natural oils, squalane, and again shea butter, which doubles as an occlusive. For oily or acne-prone skin, face washes often include actives alongside emollients to target excess oil and congestion. Other ingredients like salicylic acid, glycolic acid, AHA (alpha-hydroxy acid), BHA (beta-hydroxy acid), and niacinamide exfoliate away dead cells, regulate oil, and keep pores clear. This makes cleansers effective not only at softening but also at refining texture and reducing breakouts.

Skin Type

Matching your face wash to your skin type ensures it works with your skin’s natural needs, clears and balances your complexion, and primes your face for the products that follow.

Oily: Choose a gel-based wash that controls sebum production and excess oil with ingredients like salicylic acid or niacinamide.

Dry: Look for cream-based formulas that hydrate without stripping moisture.

Sensitive: For sensitive skin, prioritize gentle, fragrance-free cleansers that reduce the risk of irritation.

Acne-prone: Grab a cleanser with salicylic acid, benzoyl peroxide, or tea tree oil to unclog pores and fight breakouts.

How Often Should You Be Washing Your Face?

Wash your face twice a day—morning and night. Just as important, pay attention to the products you use, avoiding harsh chemicals like sulfates and parabens. Choose a gentle cleanser for everyday use and keep an exfoliating scrub for two or three times a week. Apply the scrub with light pressure—fast, hard, and rough isn’t the answer. Overdoing it can damage your skin and create new problems. And always remember: everyone’s skin type is different. What works for one person might not work for another.
