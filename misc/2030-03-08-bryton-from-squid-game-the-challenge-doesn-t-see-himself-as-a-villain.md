# Bryton From ‘Squid Game: The Challenge’ Doesn’t See Himself as a Villain

Good news for fans of Squid Game, bad news for society in general: while we wait for Season 2 of the harrowing Korean drama to arrive, streaming giant Netflix has brought its dystopian concept to life in the form of a reality show. In the first batch of episodes of Squid Game: The Challenge, now streaming, 456 people are sent to live in the same barracks as their fictional counterparts, and must compete in high-stakes versions of playground games in order to be in with a chance of winning a cash prize.

In the original series, contestants who lost the game were swiftly and brutally executed. In this version, they are simply eliminated and sent home.

One player who stood out from his hundreds of fellow competitors early on before getting ousted was Bryton, a.k.a. Playe 432. A student from South Carolina, Bryton forged alliances with several other players, but his big personality put him on other people's radar as a potential threat—and his willingness to take big risks, like in the dalgona challenge, led some to think of him as arrogant, or even a villain.

He doesn't see it that way though. His plan all along, he has revealed since his elimination, was just to be himself and enjoy the experience. He left school to take part, but far from feeling exploited, he saw Squid Game: The Challenge as an opportunity to launch his own reality TV career. All in all, unlike some of the other players on the show, he's not taking any of it too seriously.

“In the right situation, it’s okay to cry. Jesus cried, everybody cries," he told Men's Health. "But, you’re crying just because you don't get a chance to win some money. That means you’re really just there chasing a bag. We’re just playing a game, it's all luck. I'm here to have fun. I'm gonna make a name for myself. The platform that i create for myself, can be way bigger than four million dollars, but everybody was there for different reasons."

Speaking to ScreenRant, Bryton said that he doesn't regret playing the game the way he did, and that his only regret is not vouching for himself more to become team captain during the Warship game. As for anyone who is planning on auditioning for future seasons of Squid Game: The Challenge, he has a few words of guidance:

"My advice would be don't have a strategy or a plan because it's probably going to go to shit the moment that you get in there, and then you're not going to know what to do," he said. "Just go in there, have fun, and don't worry about the grand prize, because you're there to change your life and have fun. If the money comes, the money comes, but the more you chase something, the faster it runs."
