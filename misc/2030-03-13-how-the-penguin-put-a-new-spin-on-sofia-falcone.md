WITH EACH PASSING week, The Penguin just gets better and better. While Matt Reeves's 2022 film The Batman first established this new version of Gotham City—and a couple of the primary heroes (Robert Pattinson's Batman) and villains (Paul Dano's Riddler) residing in it—it's been HBO's limited series The Penguin that's painted a detailed, descriptive portrait of the comings and goings of the city's infamous criminal underworld.

That wouldn't be anywhere near as effective as it's been if we weren't getting absolutely incredible portrayals of the power players each and every week. That includes, of course, Colin Farrell's titular Oz Cobb, and incarcerated crime boss Sal Maroni (Clancy Brown), but we've got to give particular props to Cristin Milioti's Sofia Falcone.

In a way, The Penguin is Sofia's villain origin story—but told in a fairly unique way. We're introduced to her through Oz's eyes in the show's very first episode, and from his vantage point, she's a problem; a murderous, psychopathic, problem. But as the series has gone deeper and deeper into its story, the POV has shifted. We see things through Sofia's eyes—she wasn't always the villain the system painted her as, but she damn well is willing to be now. And she wants to mow through any and everyone who stands in her way, and especially those, like Oz, who have wronged her.

After Sofia gassed just about the entire Falcone family to death at the end of episode 4, "Cent'Anni," she kept just one important person alive—Johnny Viti (Michael Kelly). Johnny was a loyal foot soldier of her father, Carmine (John Turturro in The Batman and Mark Strong in The Penguin flashbacks), and now, mainly, just exists to annoy her. And so she keeps him around for, essentially, administrative purposes; he can get her the family's money, and he can help call meetings.

How does the Sofia Gigante name connect to the Batman comics?

The Penguin has done an fascinating job in paying tribute to the Batman source material while also putting its own unique spin on things. Much of the Sofia Falcone story comes from the beloved Batman arc Batman: The Long Halloween, and its sequel, Batman: Dark Victory, both ofwhich were written by Jeph Loeb and featured art by Tim Sale.

DC Comics Batman: Dark Victory

In Dark Victory—which tells the mystery of Gotham City's "Hangman" killer—Sofia actually is the Hangman. But in The Penguin, while that's her reputation, she's innocent. She still becomes a psychopathic murderer after being framed by her father and his associates and systematically tortured for ten years in Arkham Asylum, but she's not the Hangman.

Similarly, in the Batman comics, Sofia Falcone does indeed become Sofia Gigante—but it's through marriage to a gangster named Rocco Gigante. The story about Gigante being her mother's maiden name is original to The Penguin, and a great way to give Sofia more agency while also paying tribute to the source material that inspired this great character.

In an interview with IndieWire, the show's creator Lauren LeFranc put a particular emphasis on Sofia finding and putting on her late mother's fur coat (which had her name, Isabella Gigante, sewn into it).

“She wants to be able to embody who her mother was, her spirit, so she finds that mink and puts it on and becomes a Gigante," she said. "It felt very empowering to me to give Sofia that, to say I’m not a Falcone, I just obliterated the Falcones. That’s not who I am, that’s never really who I was, and now I’m going to become a Gigante and this is how I’m going to run my own crime family."
