# 33 Fantasy Movies to Watch When You Need to Escape the World

When we think about fantasy films, it might be because we're looking for a way to escape the world. They take us into realms that never existed, introduce us to magical creatures, show us unique forms of traveling, and sometimes make us pause and think about how we perceive our real world.

[Fantasy Movies](https://leanorbit.dns0663.com/blog/0365755941.html)

Movies like Where The Wild Things Are reconnected generations of people with the boundless possibility of childhood through a magical nanny. Everything Everywhere All At Once turned a mother's journey to acceptance of the life she lives and the daughter that's changing before her eyes into a multiversal deep dive down the rabbit hole of human potentiality. Marvel movies are always huge box office draws, but movies like Thor and Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings use the fantastical framework to make ancient mythology feel real.

[Movies to Watch](https://satietybrief.china-akan.com/blog/1532971537.html)

People generally think they have an idea of what a 'fantasy movie' is. But it's not all swashbuckling or spell-binding. Sometimes it could be as simple as a dream sequence or even repeating the same day over and over. If you take the time to look, even the most mundane can become fantastical. And even the most unrealistic parts of fantasy movies can connect you to real emotions.

Men's Health compiled a list of fantasy films that not only bend your imagination, but also give meaning to what exactly a fantasy film is. Sit back, grab a bowl of popcorn, suspend your disbelief, and be prepared to transport yourself to another world… maybe.

A Wrinkle in Time (2018)

Based off the Madeleine L'Engle novel of the same name, the movie follows a young girl who goes on an otherworldly quest to find her missing father, with a star-studded cast that includes Oprah Winfrey, Mindy Kaling, Reese Witherspoon, and Chris Pine.

Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring (2001)

While all the movies in this trilogy are fantastic, you need to start at the beginning to understand the magic behind them. From its illustrious sets to the all-star cast, it's a movie worthy of its lengthy running time.

Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire (2005)

Let's be honest—you've probably seen the entire franchise multiple times by now. But it's the fourth one—featuring the Triwizard Tournament and a young Robert Pattinson—that leaves more room for unique fantasy elements to shine through.

Everything Everywhere All At Once (2022)

While Marvel spent years taking us through the multiverse, it was this beautiful A24 film about a mother gaining an appreciation for the life she has through the potential she’s sacrificed. Michelle Yeoh is transcendent as disillusioned laundromat owner Evelyn Wang whose magical Bluetooth headset imbues her with the skills of her many lives across the multiverse. Half of the fantastical fun is watching Evelyn tap into the increased lung capacity of a singer to withstand smoke bombs or the coordinated agility of a sign twirler to fight off bad guys with their own shields. The other half of the fun lies in how the bizarre antics are in service of Evelyn gaining a deeper connection with her daughter Joy Wang (Stephanie Hsu). It’s no wonder a movie that ripped tears from our eyes from a silent chat between two rocks became A24’s most successful film ever

Groundhog Day (1993)

So this might not have any swashbuckling nor anything we've come to recognize as fantasy. But someone repeating the same day over and over again? That seems fantastical to me.

The Wizard of Oz (1939)

One of the most iconic films in history, The Wizard of Oz follows a young Dorothy Gale as she travels to the fantasy land Oz, where she encounters munchkins, some evil witches, and a group of friends including the Scarecrow, the Tin Man, and the Cowardly Lion.

E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial (1982)

[Movies to](https://saltbbs.com/tqlzz02f/)

Chances are at some point or another, you've seen this movie. The story of a young boy befriending an alien obsessed with Reese's Pieces had the ability to touch on real-life themes while also playing into the classic sci-fi/fantasy tropes. The film is so loved that it is considered to be one of the greatest films of all time.

Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings (2021)

The best Marvel films are ones that use the fantasy elements of superheroes as a vehicle to explore uniquely human truths grounded in reality. Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings do that better than almost any other movie in the Marvel Cinematic universe. Marvel’s first (and long overdue) film with an Asian lead explores the richness of Chinese mythology while Simu Liu kicks ass as Xu Shang-Chi in some of the best fight scenes in MCU history. It’s an educational trip with a (literal) punch that leaves an imprint on your mind for days to come.

This might not be the most successful Disney film ever made. But a Civil War soldier (played by Friday Night Lights and Waco alum Taylor Kitsch) time-traveling and ending up on Mars seems like a fantasy all on its own.

The Never-Ending Story (1984)

The movie follows a young boy, who after discovering a book, tells the story of a warrior who tries to fight off a force within a magical realm. It has all the elements of an epic fantasy film, and one that remains iconic in its genre.

The Chronicles of Narnia: The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe (2005)

Telling the story of the Pevensie siblings who try to save the magical world of Narnia, the Disney film captured the perfect amount of fantasy... within the means of a wardrobe. When you finish, don't forget to listen to "Lazy Sunday" no less than three times.

Ruby Sparks (2012)

This 2012 rom-com drama finds neurotic and sheepish writer Calvin Weir-Fields (Paul Dano) writing his girlfriend Ruby Sparks (Zoe Kazan) into existence and dealing with the moral quandary of loving someone you control. The movie sucks you in with all the inventive ways Calvin tries to write away the issues of playing God. The movie keeps you glued to the screen with the heartbreaking realization that creating your dream partner is no different than creating a personal slave.

The Princess Bride (1987)

Your wish is this movie's command. Following the story of a young Wesley who tries to save his true love Buttercup, there's a reason why the memorable quips, all-star cast and magnificent sets pushed this film to become a cult classic.

Jumanji (1995)

You might have seen the recent reboot/sequel of this movie, but the story of people getting sucked into a game is best seen in its '90s form, starring Robin Williams and a young Kirsten Dunst.

Avatar (2009)

The acting might not necessarily have been the best, but there is not one thing about this epic James Cameron film that isn't worthy of epic fantasies. The story of a paraplegic Marine who takes his brother's place on a mission to the exotic world of Pandora showed people exactly what could happen (cinematically, at least) if you dream big. You've probably seen it before, but take a little time to rewatch—because Cameron has plans for many sequels.

Ghostbusters (1984)

Outside a still formidable all-star cast including Bill Murray, Dan Aykroyd, Sigourney Weaver, Ernie Hudson, and Harold Ramis, Ghostbusters has stood the test of time as a benchmark in any cinematic ventures into the supernatural. The proton pack is still one of the coolest weapons recorded in film, and the Ghostbuster outfits have been a Halloween staple for decades (ask the Stranger Things kids).

dward Scissorhands (1990)

Johnny Depp stars as an artificial man who was left incomplete, with only scissors as his hands. As one of Tim Burton's earliest films, it makes sense how a movie about all things weird and nonsensical would be perfect if you're looking for a fantasy watch.

Beetlejuice (1988)

Following a recently deceased couple haunting the home they once lived in and getting some help from a ghost to freak out the new inhabitants, Beetlejuice was so popular that it spun off an animated series and a Broadway musical. Michael Keaton is always great, but this might be the best he's ever been—and in limited screentime, too.

Life of Pi (2012)

The book was a success, but the story of a young boy who gets stuck on a boat with a tiger in the middle of the Pacific Ocean became a very successful film, even winning four Academy Awards.

Antlers (2021)

Director Scott Cooper enlists Breaking Bad’s Jesse Plemons (Paul Meadows) and The Americans’ Keri Russell (Julia Meadows) in Antlers to confront an antlered ravenous mythical creature that burrows in its host body before terrifyingly ripping itself out in one of the most disgusting transformations you’ll ever see.
