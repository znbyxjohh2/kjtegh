Explanation

Diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN) can cause burning, tingling or stabbing pain in the hands and feet. Many people with diabetes find their daily lives impacted by nerve pain that disrupts sleep, walking and mood. Horizant (gabapentin enacarbil) is an extended-release form of gabapentin approved by the FDA to manage moderate to severe restless legs syndrome and postherpetic neuralgia. Emerging evidence suggests it may also help ease diabetic nerve pain. Below, we'll explore how Horizant works, its potential benefits and important safety considerations for using Horizant for diabetic pain.

Understanding Diabetic Nerve Pain

Diabetic peripheral neuropathy arises when high blood sugar levels damage small nerve fibers. Common symptoms include:

Burning or searing pain in the feet, calves, hands or arms

Numbness or tingling that worsens at night

Increased sensitivity to touch or temperature

Muscle weakness or coordination problems over time

Left untreated, DPN can interfere with daily activities, sleep quality and overall well-being. Early recognition and treatment are key to preventing complications such as foot ulcers.

More consistent blood levels over 12-hour dosing

How Horizant Works for Diabetic Pain

Gabapentin modulates calcium channels on nerve cells. By reducing excessive electrical activity in damaged nerves, it can:

Decrease spontaneous nerve firing that causes burning or shooting pain

Lessen central sensitization—when the spinal cord and brain overreact to normal stimuli

Improve overall pain thresholds and quality of life

Horizant's extended-release formulation helps maintain stable gabapentin levels, which may translate into smoother pain relief for those using Horizant for diabetic pain.

Evidence for Horizant in Diabetic Neuropathy

While gabapentin immediate release is widely prescribed off-label for DPN, specific trials of gabapentin enacarbil are more limited. Key findings include:

A phase II clinical study reported significant pain reduction in DPN patients taking gabapentin enacarbil versus placebo.

Patient‐reported improvements in sleep quality and nightly pain scores with extended‐release gabapentin formulas.

Comparisons to pregabalin (another gabapentinoid) suggest similar effectiveness but varying side-effect profiles.

Overall, Horizant shows promise as an alternative to immediate-release formulations, especially for patients seeking once‐ or twice‐daily dosing.

Dosage & Administration

A typical dosing schedule for Horizant when used off-label for diabetic neuropathy may mirror approved uses:

Begin with 300 mg once daily at 5 pm for 3 days.

Increase to 600 mg once daily at 5 pm, as tolerated.

Maximum dose usually does not exceed 600 mg twice daily (1,200 mg total).

Your healthcare provider will tailor the dose based on:

Pain severity and symptom pattern

Kidney function (adjustments needed for reduced clearance)

Tolerance of side effects

Never split, crush or chew the tablets—swallow them whole to preserve the extended-release properties.

Comparing Horizant to Other Treatments

Horizant for diabetic pain offers an alternative to several common options:

Tricyclic antidepressants (e.g., nortriptyline): effective but may cause dry mouth, constipation and heart rhythm issues.

Serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (e.g., duloxetine): helpful, especially with concurrent depression or anxiety.

Pregabalin: similar mechanism; requires multiple daily doses in some cases.

Opioids: effective but carry high risk of dependence, sedation and constipation.

Choosing the right therapy involves balancing efficacy, side effects, dosing convenience and personal health goals.

Who Might Benefit Most?

Horizant for diabetic pain may be suited to patients who:

Experience moderate to severe DPN pain interfering with sleep or daily function

Have struggled with multiple daily doses of other gabapentinoids

Seek a non-opioid option with proven nerve pain efficacy

Are willing to monitor side effects like drowsiness and edema

It's not recommended for pregnant or breastfeeding individuals, those with significant respiratory disease, or patients with severe kidney impairment without dose adjustments.
