🦠 HRT Steroids Combat MRSA and E. Coli

Photo by Alexander Redl on Unsplash

Researchers from the University of Kent’s School of Biosciences have discovered that a class of steroid drugs, typically used in hormone replacement therapy (HRT), can be repurposed to combat antibiotic-resistant infections like E. coli and MRSA.

These steroids target a specific protein complex called cytochrome bd, which is crucial for the survival and growth of many harmful bacteria.

By binding to this complex, the steroids can halt the growth of antibiotic-resistant E. coli and effectively kill MRSA, offering a powerful alternative to conventional antibiotics that are losing their effectiveness.

The rise of drug-resistant bacteria is a major global health threat, making this discovery incredibly significant.

This new use of steroids could revolutionize how we approach bacterial infections, providing a robust defense against resistant strains.
