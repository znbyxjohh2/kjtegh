# A Top Trainer Shares Why He (Sometimes) Trains to Failure

When you're lifting weights to get stronger and build muscle, you'll often hear that it can be beneficial to perform reps until failure. That's not only the case for when you're planning to max out on big lifts like bench presses, squats, and deadlifts—finishing off other exercises by performing reps until you can't do any more is also commonly encouraged by strength coaches.

[Trainer Shares Why He](https://tissue-renewal.china-akan.com/)

But performing reps until failure isn't just to make you exhausted for the sake of feeling like you worked really hard. Training until failure is thought of as a tried-and-true method for kickstarting the process that builds muscle, hypertrophy, and pushing your body to adapt to new challenges (that's the principle behind progressive overload). You can also reap some of these benefits without totally gassing yourself, however, which is why in other instances, coaches recommend "leaving reps in the tank" during your workouts.

[Why He Sometimes Trains](https://thymicshield.dns0663.com/)

This means you'll perform as many reps with a certain weight as it would take to tire you enough that you'd only be able to complete a few more before failure. This is helpful for times when you don't want to burn yourself out, or if you don't have a spotter available to make sure you fail safely.

Bodybuilding coach Eugene Teo believes that lifters shouldn't skip out on training to failure completely—but it's not because he's a tough guy who believes that you can only get the results you want if you push yourself beyond your limits. He broke down his thoughts on the topic in a quick YouTube video. "Training close to failure but leaving two or three reps in the tank has been shown to be just as effective for muscle building as training to failure, but it doesn't create as much fatigue," he says. "However, research has also shown that people often suck at gauging where their real limits are."

[Sometimes Trains to Failure](https://47466.com/a1k93e/)

[Trains to Failure](https://pepperedx.com/vaqvvv/)

For Teo—who says that he applies this practice to his own training on occasion—performing reps til failure is a valuable tool to keep yourself honest and continue challenging yourself. "I do recommend it as a tool to teach you exactly where true failure is so when you finish a set and say you had two or three reps left in the tank, you know you were being accurate."

[Sometimes Trains](https://copperglow.dns0663.com/)

This is important because lifters who estimate that they're approaching their threshold inaccurately are leaving potential gains on the table. Instead of getting bigger and stronger, they might wind up stuck on a training plateau. So add some sets to failure to your next training session. Just make sure you work safely, considering "failure" to be when proper form breaks down and using spotters when necessary.
