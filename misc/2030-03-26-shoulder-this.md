# Shoulder This

"Shoulder problems are very common because most men approach training with the mindset that they need to lift the heaviest weight possible," says Ivan Lewis, the strength and conditioning coach at the University of Washington. Jake Locker knows better. He builds his upper body by working on its foundation: the small muscles in his back and his rear shoulders.Alternate between performing one of these two joint-saving shoulder circuits every workout. They'll strengthen your shoulders so you can lift more weight while avoiding injury. Complete 1 set of the first exercise, rest 30 seconds, and then move to the next exercise before repeating the sequence.Circuit 1Scapular retraction (2 sets of 10 to 12 reps) Hold a barbell down in front of you at arm's length, using an overhand grip that's just beyond shoulder width. Bend at your hips and knees and lower your torso forward until it's almost parallel to the floor. With the bar still at arm's length, squeeze your shoulder blades together, hold for 1 to 2 seconds, and relax.Scapular protraction (2 sets of 10 to 12 reps) Assume a pushup position. Relax your shoulder blades so that they touch. Next, press your hands firmly into the floor so your shoulder blades separate as far as possible, without rounding your back. Hold for 1 to 2 seconds and relax. Circuit 2Scapular elevation (2 sets of 10 to 12 reps) Hold a barbell above your head using an overhand grip that's about twice shoulder width. Your arms should be completely straight. Shrug your shoulders as high as you can and hold for 1 to 2 seconds before returning to the starting position. Scapular depression (2 sets of 8 to 10 reps) Hang at arm's length from a chinup bar, using an overhand grip. Relax your shoulders so you feel your shoulder joint starting to separate. Without moving your arms, pull your shoulder blades down and together. Hold this position for 1 to 2 seconds, and return to the starting position.
