The Stages of Flu Recovery and How to Speed it Up

Influenza, or flu, is a contagious respiratory illness that typically affects your throat, nose, and lungs. The flu impacts millions of people yearly, and symptoms can range from mild to severe.

Today, we will discuss the three main stages of flu: incubation period, acute illness, and recovery. Each step of the flu recovery timeline contains slightly different symptoms, which may help you know what you should do to best care for yourself throughout the illness, and when you should seek medical care.

The Stages of Flu Recovery

The stages of the flu serve as a roadmap for what people can expect from the moment they are first exposed to the virus up through recovery.

Stage 1: Incubation period

Stage 2: Acute illness

Stage 3: Recovery

The incubation period of the flu describes the period between exposure to the virus and developing flu symptoms. The incubation time can vary slightly from person to person, but the general incubation period for the flu is between one and four days, with the average being two days.

Many factors can determine the length of the incubation period, such as:

The concentration and amount of the virus contracted

The way that the virus enters the body

The pre-existing immunity of a person (i.e. if a person is immunocompromised, if they have had a flu vaccine, etc,.)

Symptoms may start gradually over a day or two. Symptoms may include:

Body aches

Fever

Headache

Sore throat

Cough

Stuffy nose

Chills

Nausea

Vomiting

The acute illness phase is when you’re in the heart of the sickness. You may still experience all of the classic flu symptoms that started during the incubation period, or you may develop additional symptoms during this time. Typically, the symptoms last for two or three days, but some severe coughs may linger for several weeks.

During recovery, your body is in the healing process. For some people, the recovery stage starts around day six of the illness but may take up to two weeks, depending on the severity of your condition. Most of your symptoms should have subsided, but you may have some lingering mild symptoms. Resting and drinking plenty of fluids are still critical to help you regain health.

How to Speed up Flu Recovery

Throughout the day, your immune system is hard at work to keep you healthy. While flu recovery takes time, there are a few home remedies you can use to help speed up the process.

It’s important to note that learning how to handle the flu will be a different experience for each person. While some can recover in days, it can take others weeks before feeling back to normal. Utilizing the remedies mentioned below can help ward off the flu in a shorter time frame while minimizing the symptoms during recovery.

Your body requires rest for recovery. You may have small bursts of energy at different stages of flu recovery, but remember that you’re still sick and need to ease back into physical activity. Some people struggle to rest enough, especially during the last stage of flu recovery.

If you have trouble getting enough rest while sick with the flu, here are some helpful tips for getting enough rest:

Sleep alone so that you can get comfortable without disturbing someone or potentially getting them sick.

Prop yourself up so that you can breathe better and reduce coughing.

Eat or drink something warm to help soothe your throat.

Home Remedies

You’ll find a long list of home remedies at your disposal when it comes to the flu. While some are bound to work better than others, you won’t have any trouble finding something practical for you.

Herbal Teas

A go-to choice for many people, hot tea can help keep some of the persistent side effects of the flu at bay. You’re more than welcome to choose any tea you like, but a few options that are known to help include green and black tea.

If you want to give it a little added strength, consider including a bit of honey as well. This can help with how to survive the flu when dealing with an onslaught of uncomfortable symptoms.

Steam Inhalation

Your environment can positively or negatively affect your recovery process. One easy way to make yourself a bit more comfortable is by surrounding yourself with steam. There are several methods to this, such as sitting in a bathroom with a hot running shower.

Another option is to fill a pot with hot water, put your face over the steam, and put a towel over your head. This can help to loosen up mucus, making it a bit easier to breathe.

Gargling Solutions

An easy solution that only takes a few seconds, gargling warm salt water offers more benefits than you might expect. While it may not taste the best, salt water can help bring liquids to the surface, including bacteria and viruses in your throat. It isn’t an instant fix by any means, but it’s a good way to fight against the effects of the virus.

Stay Comfortable

The influenza virus tends to make you feel physically uncomfortable, both internally and externally. Unfortunately, there isn’t a single solution that helps with every bit of discomfort. Everyone’s idea of how to survive flu season is bound to vary, but there are multiple ways to make yourself as comfortable as possible until the virus dissipates.

Managing Fever

A fever is a common part of the process of dealing with the flu. While most people’s body temperatures stay within a certain range, a fever can become a big health concern if left unchecked. Staying hydrated and taking acetaminophen medications can help keep your body temperature down while you work through how to survive a flu virus.

Dealing With Body Aches

Another unavoidable aspect of the flu is persistent body aches. This tends to feel like it’s all over your body, and most people will try anything to mitigate this particular sensation.

Here are a few ways you can manage body aches when you have the flu:

Taking acetaminophen or ibuprofen medications

Self-massaging different areas of the body

Staying hydrated all throughout the day

Body aches stemming from the flu can be very persistent, or they might come and go throughout your day. The tips mentioned above require consistent application, as the body aches likely won’t stop until the virus is out of your system. Caring For a Sore Throat The severity of sore throat symptoms during the flu can range from mild to extreme. Regardless of the severity, a sore throat is never a comfortable experience, as you’re constantly reminded when you eat and every time you swallow.

Drinking hot tea with honey is a good start, and you can gargle salt water as well. Lozenges can be very helpful, too, as they soothe the throat, offering temporary relief while you work through how to handle the flu at home.

Handling Congestion

Another common symptom of the flu virus is congested sinuses. This can make it much more difficult to breathe and even cause physical pain in the face if it’s bad enough. While there isn’t always an immediate fix for this, you have plenty of options for managing it.

Once again, hot steam can be very helpful in this situation. It can aid in reducing pressure in your sinuses and loosen mucus at the same time. Your nose might run a bit, but that’s better than not being able to breathe at all. Of course, over-the-counter decongestant medications are always an option on top of other home remedies.

Manage Symptoms

Effectively managing your flu symptoms allows your body to recover as the virus runs its course through your body. While you still may not feel your best, managing the symptoms can help you get some much-needed rest.

Some general symptom management tips include:

Take over-the-counter medications to relieve fever, cough, and congestion

Suse a humidifier to help keep nasal passages open and reduce coughing

Cough drops to help soothe the throat and reduce coughing

Get plenty of rest to help with recovering from flu fatigue

Stay hydrated
