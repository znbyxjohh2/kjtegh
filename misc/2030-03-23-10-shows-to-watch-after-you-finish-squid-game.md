# 10 Shows to Watch After You Finish ‘Squid Game’

Our product picks are editor-tested, expert-approved. We may earn a commission through links on our site. Why Trust Us?

When Squid Game appeared on Netflix in mid-September, its battle-royale-style brutality—a deadly competition orchestrated for entertainment—raised, as is the case with most popular art, questions of origin: Was it really a new idea? Some viewers compared the series’ first game, “Red Light, Green Light” with a scene from As the Gods Will, a 2014 Japanese survival horror film, also a battle-royale style thriller, but, like Squid Game, involving schoolyard games.

In response to the observation, Squid Game director Hwang Dong-hyuk noted that his writing of Squid Game began long-before the 2014 Japanese release; both projects, it was suggested, grew independently. Hwang explained his own process:

“After I filmed my debut work in 2008, I went to a lot of manga and manhwa book stores. I saw a lot of survival stories so I thought it would be interesting if this genre was recreated in a Korean way. I completed the script back in 2009 but I was told that it won’t be successful since the story is cruel and the genre was unfamiliar to the public at the time. I couldn't get any investment or casting since the project was difficult to understand. So I stopped preparing it.”

Hwang eventually completed the script, which was then picked up by Netflix and given a loving home for world-wide distribution.

If Hwang was inspired by any previous property, it seemed, those properties were Japanese manga (As the Gods Will, in fact, was a Japanese manga as well, first published in 2011). Of course, the survival competition genre isn’t new or original in 2011, either. Art often builds on motifs, and while the line between inspiration and plagiarism can often be thin, it’s hard to say anyone owns a genre. (Still, Hwang has double-downed on the As the Gods Will controversy, saying in a recent interview, “It’s not really something that I wanna do, to claim ownership of this story. But if I had to say it, I would say I did it first.”)

But who really cares? Having more of a good thing isn’t a bad thing, and for viewers finishing their Squid Game binge, more is exactly what everyone wants.

So, what should you watch next?

While the first place to start will be As the Gods Will, there’s plenty more television to keep the games going. Not all the below use the game show format—because there is, of course, more to Squid Game than the bloody games—but they broach the same class-conscious themes, and they make similar observations about human behavior.

Here are 10 shows to watch after finishing Squid Game.
