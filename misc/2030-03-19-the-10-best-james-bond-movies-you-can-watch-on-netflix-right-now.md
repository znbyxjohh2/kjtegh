# The 10 Best James Bond Movies You Can Watch on Netflix Right Now

The next James Bond movie is probably not going to be called Shatterhand, whether or not it was originally going to kill poor Bond. But the fun of a new Bond movie lies in the history: 007 will live on forever because he’s been in movies longer than most of us have been alive. Here’s a look at the best Bond movies you can watch on Netflix right now.

For Your Eyes Only (1981)

By the standards of a James Bond movie, this Roger Moore joint is a darker, more realistic spy caper than the usual 007 gadgets-and-quips fare. But For Your Eyes Only’s MVP may be Carole Bouquet, who’s dynamite as a Bond girl with brains.

You Only Live Twice (1967)

Originally this was supposed to be Sean Connery’s last film, but he ultimately couldn’t resist returning later on. The fun here is that this is the Bond film that introduced Donald Pleasence as Blofeld. Incredibly, this was the first feature screenplay written by Roald Dahl, who was close friends with Ian Fleming.

This was the biggest hit of all the Bond movies at the time, and even if the underwater fights get a little tiresome, it’s still Connery ts his swaggering Bond best.

Dr. No (1962)

The first is still among the best. Here’s where Sean Connery introduced moviegoers to the concept of James Bond, creating a template for the character that every subsequent actor has had to contend with. Later installments were flashier, sexier, sillier. Dr. No just plain works as a smart, sharp spy thriller.

From Russia With Love (1963)

The first Bond sequel, and the one that realized, immediately, that the way to keep building on Bond was to make each movie a little bit more expensive and full of crazier stunts than the last one. Fun fact: This was the first movie with Desmond Llewelyn as Q, a part he would play for nearly 40 more years.

The Spy Who Loved Me (1977)

Beyond being the finest Roger Moore film, The Spy Who Loved Me also introduces us to Jaws, Bond’s fearsome, gigantic nemesis, played brilliantly by the late Richard Kiel. Just when the franchise was risking tumbling into self-parody, this installment brought back the grit and top-flight action sequences.

On Her Majesty’s Secret Service (1969)

In a parallel reality, model George Lazenby took over the reins of James Bond from Sean Connery, becoming 007 for decades to come. Instead, the little-known actor got his big break with On Her Majesty’s Secret Service, giving us a secret agent who’s tough, sexy but devoid of smirk. Lazenby only played the role once, but he made it count.

Casino Royale (2006)

It’s insane, in retrospect, how worried Bond fans were about Daniel Craig. (Because he was blonde, we guess?) Craig turned out to be precisely what Bond needed, a pumped-up, super-serious, even rather tortured hero, updating 007 at a time when he desperately needed updating. A Bond for our time, and all time.

Goldfinger (1964)

Best theme song, best villain, best evil plan: There were Bond films before Goldfinger, but this one still feels like the franchise’s Holy Grail, in which all its core components congealed perfectly. Sean Connery has never been cooler or sexier. And, yup, this is the movie where 007 first asks for a martini … shaken, not stirred.
