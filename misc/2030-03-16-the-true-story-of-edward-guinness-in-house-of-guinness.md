IF YOU'VE EVER sipped a Guinness and wondered about the Irish family who crafted the perfect pint, Netflix’s buzzy new series House of Guinness explores exactly that. From Steven Knight (Peaky Blinders), it follows the children of the late Benjamin Guinness, the heir of the brewery.

In a Succession-esque fashion, Arthur (Anthony Boyle), Anne (Emily Fairn), and Benjamin Lee Guinness II (Fionn O’Shea) try to maintain order in the company and uphold their father’s legacy in Ireland.

Yet, it’s Edward Guinness, played by British actor (and pop star Olivia Rodrigo’s boyfriend) Louis Partridge, who has been capturing viewers’s attention. Unlike his older siblings, Edward is depicted in the show as a ruthlessly creative leader. “I like to work,” he proclaims in the first episode. He leads the Guinness rebrand, including a suggestion to add the well-known Irish harp logo still used today.

While a few characters in the series, including the tough Sean Rafferty (James Norton) are fictional, Edward and his relatives were modeled after the real people. However, like any show, there are a few choices Knight makes with them for the sake of drama.

What was Edward Guinness’s role in the Guinness brewery?

Edward was Benjamin's third son and was born in 1847. He was homeschooled by a private tutor before attending Trinity College Dublin. When his father died in 1868, which is where House of Guinness starts, he became the managing director until 1889. By age 29, Edward had sole ownership of the brewery after buying out Arthur Guinness’ share for £600,000 in 1876.

He was once the richest man in Ireland, following his business idea to put two-thirds of the company on the London Stock Exchange in 1886 for £6 million. Edward retired a multi-millionaire at age 40.

Edward also remained chairman of Guinness for life, after he made it a public company. Most importantly, he commissioned the Guinness Storehouse, which remains one of Dublin’s most popular tourist attractions today.

Was Edward Guinness wealthy?

Even with all of Edward’s wealth, he was a dedicated philanthropist. He contributed almost £1 million to housing projects, known as the Guinness Trust. It was founded in 1890, and later became the Iveagh Trust in Dublin in 1903. Today, it provides over 10% of social housing in the city.

Edward donated £250,000 to the Lister Institute in 1898, which was the first medical research charity in 1898. He co-funded London’s Radium Institute in 1908 and sponsored physics and botany buildings at his alma mater.

He obtained a seat on the House of Lords in 1891. Edward was also the 1st Earl of Iveagh when he died in 1927 at age 79 in London.

What was Edward Guinness’ personality like?

The Guinnesses: The Untold Story of Ireland’s Most Successful Family

Edward was described as a “stickler for detail, to the point of obsession” in the 2009 biography, The Guinnesses: The Untold Story of Ireland’s Most Successful Family, and he “noted the spending of every single penny in his diary.” The book’s author, Joe Joyce, wrote that he still kept “trying to compete with his older brother,” Arthur, despite his continued success.

The House of Guinness show taps into Edward and Arthur’s dramatic tension by setting up a rivalry in the very first episode. Their aunt approaches them, comparing the brothers to Ogma and Dagda, sons of a god in Irish mythology. Or, as the poised and cold Edward notes, “One can be trusted. The other cannot be trusted.”
