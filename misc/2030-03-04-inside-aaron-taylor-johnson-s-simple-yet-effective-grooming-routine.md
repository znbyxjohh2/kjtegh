ON THE SURFACE, Aaron Taylor-Johnson is anything but simple. The 33-year-old actor is known for his kaleidoscope of roles and a chameleon-like ability to completely immerse himself in them. Whether he’s playing a sleazy hitman in Bullet Train, a struggling drug addict in A Million Little Pieces, a ruthless anti-superhero in the forthcoming Kraven The Hunter or, as many (wishfully) speculated, possibly slipping into 007’s famous tuxedo, what makes Taylor-Johnson stand out is his ability to completely disappear into the roles he plays.

It’s no surprise, then, that he thinks (a lot) about how and why his characters groom the way they do. But what he does for himself is surprisingly simple. He’s not lugging a big bag of products around the world with him, but instead giving his body what it needs when it needs it. That self-awareness is what makes him the ideal partner to Giorgio Armani, who today announced that Taylor-Johnson is the new face of the iconic Acqua di Gio fragrance collection. “Acqua di Gio embodies a way of understanding masculinity that is at once free, sensitive, and adventurous,” Armani wrote in a press release. "These are all characteristics that Aaron expresses with his presence and physicality."

Along with new campaign images featuring Taylor-Johnson, the classic Acqua di Gio bottle has now been redesigned into a simpler, modernized version featuring frosted glass and, most importantly, the ability to be refilled. Simple? Yes—and that’s the point. Acqua di Gio’s iconic, elegant simplicity is unchanged at its core, yet modernized. Who better to illustrate that than Taylor-Johnson? Men’s Health caught up with the actor to find out exactly how simple his grooming routine is, what grooming ritual he never skips, and how he, personally, wears that Acqua di Gio.

Men's Health: What is your daily grooming routine like?

Aaron Taylor-Johnson: I don’t really do that much on a daily basis. I’ve learned that less is more for me. I was just in Jamaica, and the water there was very good for my skin and my hair. I didn’t really want to do much to mess with this so I went with it. On most days I’ll really just use a good moisturizing oil for both my skin and my hair and that’s it. For me, it’s about simplicity. If your lips are chapped, you’re dehydrated. Drink some water.

Have you picked up any grooming tricks from groomers on set that you still do?

The one piece of grooming for me is shaving. I like to take my time shaving—to make a little ritual of it. I try not to rush through it because it’s such an important part of grooming, and even though the rest of my routine is minimal, I like to savor a shave and make sure I do it right.

Your characters always have such a distinct look. Does grooming play a role in your character development?

Oh yeah, it plays a huge role. In Bullet Train for instance, we wanted my look to be very range-y, like a featherweight boxer, so I did a lot of boxing and training to give my body that look. It really leaned me out, so we decided on that mustache to accentuate the lines of my face and drive that look home. Fragrance actually plays a part, too. Sometimes I'll wear a fragrance that has the vibe of my character to help me get into his head.
