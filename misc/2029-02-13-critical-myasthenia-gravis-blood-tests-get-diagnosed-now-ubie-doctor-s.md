Internal Medicine

Blood tests for myasthenia gravis detect specific autoantibodies—most commonly anti-acetylcholine receptor (AChR), anti-MuSK, and anti-LRP4 antibodies—that mistakenly attack proteins at the neuromuscular junction, disrupting communication between nerves and muscles. Identifying these antibodies helps confirm the diagnosis and guide treatment.

Explanation

To diagnose myasthenia gravis, doctors often use blood tests to find certain antibodies. These antibodies can attack parts of the muscles and nerves, making it hard for the body to move properly. Main Tests:

[Blood Tests](https://immunopeptide.xianfengchem.com/)

Acetylcholine Receptor Antibodies (AChR): This is the most common test. It checks for antibodies that attack the acetylcholine receptors, which are important for muscle movement.

Muscle-Specific Kinase (MuSK) Antibodies: If the AChR test is negative, doctors might look for MuSK antibodies. These are found in some people with myasthenia gravis, especially when AChR antibodies are not present.
