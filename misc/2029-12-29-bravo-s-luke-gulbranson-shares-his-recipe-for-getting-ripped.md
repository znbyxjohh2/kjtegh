# Bravo's Luke Gulbranson Shares His 'Recipe for Getting Ripped'

LUKE GULBRANSON is an unabashed striver among the group on Bravo’s Winter House and Summer House. While he now lives in New York City, the Minnesota native flaunts an outdoorsy fitness routine and never misses a chance to promote one of his numerous businesses, such as the Renaissance man-inspired retail brand R_Co. His playboy behavior on Bravo’s reality shows can be controversial, but his hobbies off the screen seem pretty laid-back, even possibly… humble.

Here, we asked Gulbranson rapid-fire questions about his personal life. He obliged, revealing his workout favorites (including jump roping and jump squats), his romantic tips (don’t slack on your GIF-sending game), and his obsession with, yes, the cello.

MH: What have been the biggest surprises going into reality TV?

LG: Becoming really good friends with some of the people that I was in a house with. You're in a situation where some people are nothing like you. And then sometimes you're like, “Holy shit, now you're one of my best friends.” I think we've grown up as individuals. You're forced to face things that outside of that situation, you probably don't face. You learn a lot about yourself.

Living a jet-setting lifestyle is your job now. How do you stay in shape wherever you go?

I was an athlete growing up. I played football and hockey. It’s just who I am. When I am traveling, I always have to find ways to do it. And it's interesting, especially with Covid. I'll find a log in the woods and use that on my back for squats. One thing that I always have when I travel is a jump rope because jump roping is great cardio. It’s great to be able to mix in circuit training with like four-minute rounds of jump roping into your jump squats and your pushups. You're trying to hit all your body parts.

What does your ideal jump rope workout look like?

Here is a breakdown of a jump rope workout I do while I’m traveling or off in the woods hiking, camping, etc.

[I do] a total of 20 minutes jumping with the rope. I do 5 sets of 4-minute rounds. After each round, I begin my bodyweight exercises. For these 4-minute rounds, I like to get some good tunes on my headphones. Something I can find a beat to. A good rhythm helps your rope skills.

I proceed with one-leg jumps, counting up to 10 on each leg, then back down from 10 on each leg. Then 20 with both legs. Onto alternating feet for a count of 20. Then double-bounce jumps, alternating feet for a count of 20. Then alternating heel taps for a count of 20. Next, double heel taps alternating for a count of 20. Followed by a few crossovers, left to right, right to left. Then two-foot crossovers into high knees for 15-20 seconds. I’m usually gassed by this point, but battle into 5 one-leg jumps, up and down the count, repeating until I hit 4 minutes. I always burn the last 15 seconds doing fast feet with high knees and double jumps.

Your bodyweight exercises in between rounds of jump rope include push-ups, jump squats, wide grip lat pullups, biceps pullups, triceps extensions, shoulder raises, mountain climbers, V-up twists, crunches, planks...

You do the rope-plus-bodyweight circuit for a total of 5 circuits. This usually takes about 45 minutes total. Oh, and no big breaks. You go from one movement to the next, then take 1 minute and 30 seconds to rest at the end of the circuit. This way you’re getting a good cardio session in while you’re attacking your muscles.

We know how you work out when you're camping, but what about fitness at the airport?

One thing that I try to do if I'm not rushing for my flight is, instead of taking those moving walkways, I take stairs. I do everything I can to make sure I'm giving my body movement. That’s why I don't have an electric bike. I pedal.

And your most dreaded exercise?

Oh my God. Okay. There’s this machine called the reACT machine. My legs want to fall off when I'm doing it. But I love doing it because I love to exhaust myself and grind it out at the gym.
