Yes, poor sleep can disrupt your hormones and reproductive health. Sleep deprivation alters the release of key hormones like cortisol, melatonin, estrogen, testosterone, and luteinizing hormone (LH), which regulate ovulation, menstrual cycles, fertility, and libido. Chronic sleep loss may lead to irregular periods, reduced fertility, and lower sex drive in both men and women.

Explanation

Sleep deprivation can significantly impact hormonal health. When you don't get enough sleep, your body's hormonal balance can be disturbed. For example, sleep loss can lead to higher levels of cortisol, a stress hormone, which might affect how your body manages stress and energy.

Moreover, sleep deprivation can also influence other hormones involved in metabolism and growth. The body's ability to release hormones like insulin and those related to appetite can be altered. This means that not getting enough sleep might lead to changes in how hungry you feel and how your body uses energy from food.
