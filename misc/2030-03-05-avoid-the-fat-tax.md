# Avoid the Fat Tax

Carrying around excess pounds costs you—literally, if you live in Alabama and work for the state.

Alabama is planning to charge all 37,527 state employees an extra $25 a month in health-care costs starting next year. Employees who pass the mandatory health screening with a clean bill of health will get that money back, but if tests show high blood pressure, cholesterol, and glucose levels, or if they have a BMI of more than 35, they will be charged.

Employees screened with these risk factors can waive the fee by seeing their physician, enrolling in a state-sponsored exercise or nutritional class, or losing the excess weight in a year.

National headlines have been quick to call Alabama’s new health screening program a “fat tax,” but that’s not the case, says William Ashmore, CEO for the State Employees Insurance Board. He says it’s a worksite wellness discount program that credits people for making healthier lifestyle choices.

“The main thing we’re hoping for is to identify at-risk people and provide the means for them to manage their health issues,” he says. “Many people do not know they have hypertension or are diabetic. We want to try to make sure every employee is given an incentive to figure out what risks they might have.”

Ashmore says a BMI of more than 40 correlates to being around 100 pounds overweight, and 7.3 percent of Alabama state employees fall into this category.

Jim McVay, Ph.D., director of health promotion in chronic disease with the Alabama department of public health, says he hopes this program works to motivate people to be healthier.
