# The Strongman Challenge

Before you go lifting massive tires and Atlas stones, consider strength training like a strongman competitor in the gym, with regular weights.

The barbell row requires maximum effort from the largest muscles of your upper body, making it a great test of strength, says C.J. Murphy, M.F.S., a strongman competitor and gym owner.

Murphy sets the bar high: A strong guy, he says, should be able to row the equivalent of his body weight six to 10 times, using the technique below. He should know: He flips 850-pound tires for fun.

Instructions: Perform each strength training workout once a week, resting at least 2 days after a session. Rest 2 minutes between sets for the first move in each workout, and no more than 60 seconds between sets for the remaining exercises.

Barbell 45-Degree Row

Stand with your knees slightly bent, holding a barbell overhand against your thighs. Squeeze your shoulder blades and push your hips back until your torso is at a 45-degree angle. Pull the bar up your thighs, driving your elbows back and down and lifting your chest. Lower the bar. Do five sets of six reps.

Stand holding a barbell down in front of you at arm's length, with an overhand shoulder-width grip. Without moving your arms, raise your shoulders as high as you can. Return to the starting position. Do four sets of eight repetitions.

Lie with your knees bent and your feet flat on the floor, anchored under a sturdy object, such as a sofa. Hold your hands behind your ears. Slowly lift your upper body, bringing your chest toward your knees. Lower back down slowly. Perform as many sets as needed to complete 100 reps.

Dumbbell Single-Arm Row

Holding a dumbbell in your right hand, place your left hand and left knee on a bench. Hold the weight with your arm just in front of your shoulder. Use your upper-back muscles to pull the dumbbell up and back toward your hip. Pause, then slowly lower the weight. Do four sets of six reps.

Hammer Curl

Hold dumbbells at arm's length with a neutral grip (palms facing each other). Keeping your upper arms against your sides, curl the weights toward your shoulders. Squeeze your biceps at the top, then lower the weights. Do three sets of 12 reps.
