# How One of Baseball’s Best Sluggers Can Play Hard and Still Drink Beer

It's Opening Day Thursday, and three-time all-star Anthony Rizzo of the Chicago Cubs is ready for his eighth year of the grind that is life as a Major League Baseball player.

Every season, MLB teams play a schedule of 162 games over seven months, traveling to stadiums all around the country (and Toronto). A nonstop nationwide tour complete with long nights on the road and games during the day can take a toll on anyone's body, but at 28, Rizzo has mastered the challenging lifestyle as a certified veteran. He has a World Series win and several accolades under his belt, including the MLB's most recent Roberto Clemente Award for work off the field with young cancer patients (Rizzo overcame his own bout with Hodgkin's lymphoma shortly after being drafted by the Boston Red Sox, in 2008).

So how does Rizzo manage to keep up a regular training routine during the season, when games, travel, and his work in the community dominate his schedule? He says maintaining a work-to-life balance is the key to that success, even during the most hectically busy periods. That means that he'll still hit the gym on game days—but he won't totally give up certain aspects of a "normal" life.

Specifically, beer.

"I love beer," Rizzo tells MensHealth.com. "During the season, I really try to cut it out at all costs, because if you drink a couple of beers here and there they add up. [If you do drink], it’s about being professional. You have to show up the next day and be ready to perform."

Here are a few of his tips to keeping well-balanced lifestyle on the road.

Make time for your workouts, no matter what

Even though Rizzo has a million things to do during the season, maintaining a consistent strength training routine is essential for balance. Sometimes that takes extra effort, but it's worth the work because he prefers to make the gym his refuge off the field, where some MLB players conduct all their workouts.

"I like working out at a normal gym on the road to get out and away from the baseball atmosphere and feel like a normal human being." he says.

"I’ll wake up, chug a water, get a breakfast and go to the gym around 10 a.m. on the road or 11 a.m. at home." With his training session out of the way, Rizzo is more prepared to perform on the field later in the day.

Focus on the basics

Rizzo says his in-season workouts range anywhere from 45 minutes to an hour and a half—but he doesn't push too hard. "During the season, I’m a little more reserved, because, at the end of the day, the last thing I want to do is tweak something in the gym before a game."

He focuses on core lifts, like squats and deadlifts, and hits the chest, too. As a slugger with four consecutive 30 home run seasons dating back to 2014, he also puts major emphasis on practical movement training and core strength to keep his swing fluid and powerful. "Core is my go-to all the time," he says. "I try to make sure my core is tight and intact, so everything else can fire the right way."

Remember to step outside of your comfort zone

While Rizzo sticks to the basics for his training during the season, he doesn't skip out on any exercises specifically to avoiding building up the wrong type of muscle. He's still game to add moves to his repertoire that others might skip.

"I try to look at it as ‘I’m Anthony and baseball is my job, but working out is something I enjoy doing,’" he says. "I’ll never limit myself to not doing anything because of baseball."

That includes one activity that you might not expect a 6'3'', 240 lb. slugger to endorse. "Pilates is something that I’ll do in the off-season and even during season to make sure I’m staying loose."

Rest and recovery are key

Rizzo knows that there is a limit to how much he can train during the busy season, so there are other aspects of his training plan that can be even more important.

He's adamant about getting the proper amount of rest for recovery every night, no matter what. He considers it to be paramount to his job as a pro baseball player. "Sometimes it’s not easy to get into bed after a big game or when you travel and get in at four in the morning, but we’re professional," he says. "Sleep is definitely an essential part."

An even bigger concern for Rizzo is staying properly hydrated. He says hydration the "biggest thing that I try to hold myself accountable for," and endorses a sports drink, BodyArmor. "I’m pretty much always drinking water or a sports drink," he says. "Whether it’s at the beginning of the workout or the end, I feel like if I drink enough water, I'll have enough energy."

Practice portion control

Rizzo doesn't follow a strict diet during the season, which helps him to manage the seven month long march to the playoffs in October. Instead, he tries to approach his nutrition as a realist. "I try to really focus on not over-eating during the season," he says. "Once you train yourself to do that, you’re way better off."

Still, he allows himself to indulge within reason to help make the long season just a little more enjoyable. "I’m very conscious of what I put in my body, but I also enjoy eating pizza and Taco Bell," he says. "It’s about doing all of that in moderation."
