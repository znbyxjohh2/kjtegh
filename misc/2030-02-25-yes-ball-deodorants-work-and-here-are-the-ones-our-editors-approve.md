ANY MAN CAN tell you that the groin area can get a little funky, especially when you start factoring in intense workouts, high heat indexes and, well, just daily life. It’s one of those places on the body that tends to get sweaty quickly and with that sweat comes odor. "It is already a warm and often moist environment that is friendly to bacteria and yeast," says dermatologist Robert Finney, MD, founder of Soho Skin and Hair Restoration in New York City. "Overgrowth of both can contribute to odor." Even though sweat and odor down there is a fact of life, it doesn’t mean that you have to just accept it. To help control both, ball deodorants are one of your most effective solutions.

"Deodorants are designed to neutralize body odor," says dermatologist Joshua Zeichner, MD, Director of Cosmetic and Clinical Research, Department of Dermatology at Mount Sinai in New York City. Completely separate from antiperspirants, which use aluminum to stop sweating, deodorants "are aluminum-free products that contain a variety of different ingredients to mask odor and lower levels of bacteria on the skin that break down sweat causing a foul smell," he adds.

Many deodorants these days are formulated to be safe to use on all parts of your body and some are even designed specifically for your groin. Using one can help keep your balls feeling (and smelling) fresher, cleaner, and more comfortable throughout the day. "Anyone who has odor they are concerned about" can benefit from a ball deodorant, says Dr. Finney, who advises applying it in the morning and letting it dry before getting dressed. Whether you use a spray, lotion, or stick, a ball deodorant should go to work immediately and keep you fresh all day.

What to Consider

Ingredients

In a deodorant, you’ll want to look for two main things: odor-fighting ingredients and moisture-absorbing ingredients. On the odor-fighting front, this could be anything from zinc neodecanoate, magnesium hydroxide, plant starches and some pre or probiotics.

Other ingredients like arrowroot powder, tapioca starch, and baking soda will also help absorb moisture (if you have sensitive skin, avoid baking soda since it can cause skin irritation). You’ll also want to stay away from talc, which has been linked to certain types of cancer. In addition to those, Dr. Finney says to look for natural ingredients that benefit your skin like vitamin E and aloe vera.

Format

Deodorants come in a variety of forms like sticks, lotions, balms, and sprays. Which form you choose is more of a personal choice. Sprays are often easier to use and don’t require you to rub them in, unlike creams and lotions. But if you get irritation from chafing, you may want to avoid them, says Dr. Finney, since they will "not be as helpful [at minimizing chafing] as a cream."

Scent

Of course, a big part of deodorant is odor control and along with that comes the scent. Many deodorants use scent to bolster any odor-controlling ingredients and help keep areas fresh and smelling good. What scent you choose, naturally, is a personal preference and you should always pick a deodorant with a scent you like. If you have very sensitive skin, fragrance may cause some irritation, so consider a fragrance-free option.
