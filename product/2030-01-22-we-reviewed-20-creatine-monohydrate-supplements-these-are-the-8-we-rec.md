When to Consult Your Doctor

Before starting any supplement, it’s a good idea to consult your doctor if you have any underlying health conditions or concerns. Here are a few situations in which it would be wise to speak with a healthcare professional before trying creatine:

Kidney issues: Creatine is processed through the kidneys, so if you have any preexisting kidney issues (such as kidney disease or impaired kidney function), speak with your healthcare provider before starting creatine.

Medications: If you are taking medications, particularly those that impact kidney function or heart health or act as diuretics (“water pills”), it is crucial to consult your doctor before starting creatine supplements, as there is a potential for interactions.

Pregnancy or breastfeeding: If you’re pregnant or breastfeeding, you should consult your healthcare provider before starting creatine supplements, as there is not enough research to definitively confirm its safety in this population.

What to Look for When Buying Creatine Monohydrate Supplements

With the abundance of creatine supplements on the market, there are a few factors to consider when choosing the right one for you:

Third-party testing: This ensures that the product meets safety, potency, and purity standards, so you know exactly what you’re putting into your body. Organizations that offer third-party testing include:

NSF International is a product testing, inspection, and certification organization for public health standards.

NSF Certified for Sport is an independent third-party certification program helping athletes make safer decisions when choosing sports supplements.

United States Pharmacopeia is an independent, scientific nonprofit organization that aims to help protect patient safety in medicines, dietary supplements, and foods worldwide.

ConsumerLab.com evaluates consumer products related to health, wellness, and nutrition, providing independent test results and information to help consumers and healthcare professionals identify the best products.

Informed Choice is a global quality assurance and third-party supplement testing program designed to minimize the risk of dietary supplement products being contaminated with prohibited and potentially harmful substances.

Clean Label Project focuses on food and product safety issues consumers increasingly care about, such as the presence of heavy metals, pesticide residues, plasticizers, and other chemicals of concern, as well as truth in labeling.

Clinically studied ingredients: Using clinically studied ingredients is crucial because it helps ensure safety, efficacy, and reliability. Clinically backed ingredients offer evidence-based support, reducing the risk of adverse effects and ensuring that the product delivers measurable benefits. As Lemke states, “Creatine monohydrate is the best form — it’s safe, effective, and backed by decades of research.”

Price: Creatine supplement prices can increase dramatically as various ingredients are added or the form changes. For example, a single-serving creatine stick will cost more than one serving from a jug. Capsules may cost more than powder, as you will go through capsules quicker than scoops of powder, merely due to the recommended serving size.

Form: Whether it’s a powder, capsule, or gummy, choose a format you’re comfortable with. This is especially important if the recommended dose is more than once per day. You want to choose a supplement you will take.

Special dietary needs: For example, if you are vegan or vegetarian, be sure the supplement you choose uses plant-based ingredients to complement your lifestyle.

FAQ

Creatine offers several key benefits, primarily its ability to enhance physical performance during short bursts of high-intensity exercise, such as sprinting, weight lifting, and interval training. Creatine works to increase the body’s primary energy source, which can help improve strength, power, and endurance.
