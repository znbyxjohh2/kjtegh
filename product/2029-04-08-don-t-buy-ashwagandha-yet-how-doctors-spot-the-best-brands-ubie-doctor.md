Explanation

Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) is one of the most studied adaptogens used to support stress management, sleep, cognitive function and overall vitality. Two of the most popular standardized extracts on the market are KSM-66 and Sensoril. Here's how your doctor evaluates these brands to decide which one may be right for you.

1. Understanding the Basics

Before comparing brands, it's important to know:

Ashwagandha's traditional uses: stress relief, improved sleep quality, enhanced mood, support for immune and reproductive health.

Adaptogens in general help the body adapt to physical and mental stressors.

Standardization ensures consistent levels of active compounds (withanolides).

2. Extraction Process & Plant Part Used

Brand extraction methods and plant parts affect potency and safety:

KSM-66

Extracted from roots only.

Uses a water-based, alcohol-free process.

Standardized to a minimum of 5% total withanolides (measured by HPLC).

Sensoril

Extracted from roots and leaves.

Standardized to a minimum of 10% total withanolides, of which at least 32% are withanolide glycosides.

Often uses a combination of water and alcohol extraction.

Why it matters:

Roots tend to have fewer withaferin A compounds, which are more cytotoxic.

Leaf-based extracts may deliver higher withanolide levels but can carry a slightly different safety profile.

3. Clinical Evidence & Research

Doctors rely on peer-reviewed studies to guide recommendations:

Anxiety & Stress: In a randomized control trial, participants taking 300 mg twice daily reported significant reductions in stress and cortisol levels compared to placebo. (PubMed ID: 30457927)

Cognitive Function: Studies suggest improvements in reaction time, memory tasks and processing speed at doses of 300–600 mg/day.

Sexual Health: Evidence indicates enhanced testosterone levels and sexual performance in both men and women.

Stress & Sleep: In clinical trials with 125–250 mg twice daily, participants experienced improved sleep quality and reduced perceived stress. (PubMed ID: 27168716)

Endurance & Energy: Some studies show benefits in VO₂ max and fatigue reduction in healthy adults.

Doctors look at:

Size and quality of the study (number of participants, duration).

Primary outcomes (stress scales, cortisol, sleep indices).

Safety data (adverse events reported).

4. Safety & Tolerability

Both extracts have strong safety records, but nuances exist:

Well tolerated in doses up to 600 mg/day for 8–12 weeks.

Mild side effects (if any): occasional gastrointestinal discomfort, drowsiness.

Typical clinical doses 250–500 mg/day for 8–12 weeks.

Mild side effects: headaches, digestive upset, very rare allergic reactions.

5. Dosage Forms & Practicality

Doctors factor in patient preferences and daily routine:

Capsules/Tablets – Most common, precise dosing.

Powders – Flexible dosing, can mix into shakes or foods.

Typical daily dosages:

KSM-66: 300–600 mg/day, often split into two doses.

Sensoril: 125–250 mg twice daily.

6. Quality Assurance & Certifications

Organic & Non-GMO – Preferred by some patients for purity concerns.

Your doctor may review certificates of analysis (CoAs) or manufacturer quality audits.

8. How Doctors Evaluate Brand Claims

When a supplement label makes lofty claims, doctors check:

Published Human Trials – Not just animal or in vitro data.

Dosage Alignment – Clinical studies vs. recommended dose on label.

Active Compound Verification – Are withanolide levels independently tested?

Adverse Event Reporting – Has the brand logged side effects anywhere?

10. Practical Tips for Patients

Buy from reputable retailers (pharmacies, known health-food stores).

Verify third-party testing logos on the label.

Keep a simple log: dose, time taken, any benefits or side effects.

Allow 4–8 weeks to assess real benefits; adaptogens often work gradually.

Discuss any new supplement with your healthcare provider, especially if on medications.
