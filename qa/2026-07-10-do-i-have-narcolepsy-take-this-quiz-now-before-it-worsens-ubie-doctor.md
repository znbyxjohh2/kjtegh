How to know if you have narcolepsy: Narcolepsy is characterized by excessive daytime sleepiness, and common signs include a sudden urge to sleep despite a full night's rest, or falling asleep unexpectedly while eating, working, or talking. Other key symptoms may include sudden muscle weakness (cataplexy), sleep paralysis, and vivid hallucinations when falling asleep or waking.

A definitive narcolepsy diagnosis requires a healthcare provider, who will conduct a personalized medical assessment and may recommend specialized sleep testing such as the Multiple Sleep Latency Test (MSLT), which measures how quickly you fall asleep in a quiet daytime environment.

Because narcolepsy shares symptoms with many other conditions—like sleep apnea, depression, or thyroid disorders—identifying the likely cause early can save you time, money, and unnecessary worry. Narcolepsy is a sleep disorder that can make people feel very sleepy during the day. If you are wondering if you have narcolepsy, you can start by asking yourself if you have the sudden urge to sleep even after getting a full night's sleep, or if you fall asleep in the middle of eating, working, or talking with someone. Another question is about how you feel after a nap; specifically, after a nap, does the feeling of alertness after you wake up napping quickly change to sleepiness?

To be certain about a diagnosis of narcolepsy, you will need to consult a healthcare provider. This healthcare provider will provide a personalized medical assessment of your symptoms. Also, the healthcare provider may recommend some tests, such as sleep test called polysomnography (PSG), which is performed in a healthcare facility designed to conduct sleep tests. Patients undergoing the PSG test stay overnight in the healthcare facility to complete this test.

Another part of testing for narcolepsy, the Multiple Sleep Latency Test (MSLT) (which helps measure how fast you can fall asleep during the day when you're in a quiet place), often takes place a few hours after PSG testing is finished. A healthcare provider may also recommend additional tests, depending on your symptoms. If you're experiencing excessive daytime sleepiness or other concerning symptoms, you can take a free symptom checker assessment to help identify potential causes and determine if you should seek medical care.
