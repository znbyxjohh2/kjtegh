# Abdominal Pain and Blood? Don’t Ignore These Critical Causes | Ubie Doctor's Note

Published on: 1/29/2026

What causes abdominal pain and blood together?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Abdominal pain with blood may signal issues in the digestive, urinary, or reproductive tract. Common causes include infections, hemorrhoids, fissures, inflammatory bowel disease, ulcers, diverticular disease, kidney stones, UTIs, or endometriosis. Less commonly, it can indicate cancer. The blood's color, amount, and accompanying symptoms help identify the cause.

Seek urgent care for heavy or ongoing bleeding, black tarry stools, vomiting blood, severe or worsening pain, dizziness, fainting, or fever with marked tenderness.

Because abdominal pain with blood has many possible causes—ranging from minor to serious—identifying your specific pattern of symptoms is the critical first step. Explanation

Abdominal pain together with blood can be worrying, but it does not always mean something dangerous. The meaning depends on where the blood is coming from, how much there is, and what other symptoms are present. Understanding the possible causes can help you know when to monitor symptoms and when to speak to a doctor promptly.

This article explains the most common and serious reasons abdominal pain and blood may occur together, using clear language and medically credible explanations.

What does "abdominal pain and blood" usually mean?

When people report abdominal pain with blood, they are usually describing one of the following:

Blood in the stool (bright red, dark red, or black and tarry)

Blood in vomit

Blood in urine

Blood from the rectum

Blood seen during bowel movements along with abdominal pain

The color, amount, and timing of the blood give important clues about its cause.

Common causes of abdominal pain and blood

1. Gastrointestinal infections

Infections of the digestive tract can inflame the intestines and cause bleeding.

Typical features:

Crampy abdominal pain

Diarrhea (sometimes bloody)

Fever

Nausea or vomiting

Why bleeding happens:Bacteria or viruses irritate the lining of the intestines, making it fragile and prone to bleeding.

These infections are often linked to contaminated food or water and usually improve with supportive care, though some require medical treatment.

2. Hemorrhoids and anal fissures

These are very common and usually not dangerous causes of blood with abdominal pain.

Signs include:

Bright red blood on toilet paper or in the bowl

Pain or discomfort during bowel movements

Mild lower abdominal discomfort from constipation or straining

Why bleeding happens:Straining causes swollen blood vessels or small tears in the anal area.

This type of bleeding is usually minor, but ongoing symptoms should still be evaluated.

3. Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)

Conditions such as Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis cause long-term inflammation of the digestive tract.

Symptoms may include:

Persistent abdominal pain

Bloody diarrhea

Fatigue

Weight loss

Urgency to use the bathroom

Why bleeding happens:Chronic inflammation damages the intestinal lining, leading to ulcers and bleeding.

IBD is not life-threatening in the short term, but it does require ongoing medical care.

4. Stomach or intestinal ulcers

Ulcers are open sores that develop in the stomach or small intestine.

Possible symptoms:

Burning or gnawing abdominal pain

Blood in stool (often dark or black)

Nausea or bloating

Why bleeding happens:The ulcer erodes blood vessels in the digestive tract.

Ulcers are often related to medications like NSAIDs or bacterial infection and are very treatable when diagnosed early.

5. Diverticular disease

Small pouches called diverticula can form in the colon and sometimes bleed or become infected.

Common signs:

Sudden lower abdominal pain (often left-sided)

Changes in bowel habits

Why bleeding happens:Weak areas in the colon wall can rupture blood vessels.

Bleeding can be mild or heavy, but many cases resolve with medical guidance.

6. Kidney stones or urinary tract issues

If blood is coming from the urine rather than stool, the cause may be urinary rather than digestive.

Sharp abdominal or side pain

Pain that comes in waves

Blood in urine (pink, red, or brown)

These conditions are often painful but treatable.

7. Endometriosis (in people with a uterus)

Endometriosis can cause abdominal pain and bleeding that may be mistaken for digestive issues.

Possible signs:

Lower abdominal or pelvic pain

Pain during periods

Blood in stool during menstruation

Digestive discomfort

Why bleeding happens:Endometrial tissue outside the uterus can bleed and irritate nearby organs.

This condition is chronic but manageable with medical care.

8. Cancers of the digestive system

While less common, cancers of the colon, stomach, or intestines can cause abdominal pain and bleeding.

Ongoing abdominal pain

Blood in stool (sometimes hidden)

Unexplained weight loss

It's important to note that most cases of abdominal pain and blood are not cancer, but persistent or unexplained symptoms should always be checked.

When abdominal pain and blood may be serious

You should speak to a doctor urgently if abdominal pain and blood occur with:

Heavy or ongoing bleeding

Black, tarry stools

Severe or worsening abdominal pain

Dizziness or fainting

Fever and severe tenderness

Vomiting blood

These signs can indicate internal bleeding or serious inflammation.

How doctors determine the cause

To understand why abdominal pain and blood are happening together, a doctor may:

Ask detailed questions about symptoms

Perform a physical exam

Order blood tests

Request stool or urine tests

Use imaging such as ultrasound or CT scans

Recommend endoscopy or colonoscopy if needed

The goal is to identify the source of bleeding and treat it appropriately.

What you can do now

If you are experiencing abdominal pain with blood:

Track when the pain occurs and what makes it better or worse

Note the color and amount of blood

Avoid self-diagnosing or ignoring persistent symptoms

Stay hydrated unless advised otherwise

The importance of medical guidance

Abdominal pain with blood can range from minor and temporary to serious and life-threatening. While many causes are treatable and manageable, only a qualified healthcare professional can determine what's truly going on.
