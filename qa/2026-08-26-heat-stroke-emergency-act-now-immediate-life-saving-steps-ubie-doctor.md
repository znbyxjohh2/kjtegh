What should I do when I have heat stroke?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Heat stroke is a life-threatening medical emergency that requires immediate action. If you suspect heat stroke, move to a cool environment, remove excess clothing, and begin rapid cooling by applying cold water, ice packs, or fans to the skin. Hydrate with cool fluids if the person is conscious, and call 911 without delay.

Recognizing heat stroke early can prevent organ damage or death. Symptoms often include high body temperature, confusion, rapid pulse, and hot, dry skin. Because symptoms can overlap with other serious conditions, understanding what you're experiencing is critical. Recognizing Heat Stroke:

Heat stroke occurs when the body overheats, typically due to prolonged exposure to high temperatures or strenuous physical activity in hot weather. Symptoms may include:

High body temperature (above 104°F or 40°C)

Altered mental state or confusion

Prevention Tips:

To prevent heat stroke in the future, consider the following strategies:

Stay hydrated by drinking plenty of fluids, especially in hot weather.

Avoid strenuous activities during the hottest parts of the day.

Wear lightweight, loose-fitting clothing.

Take regular breaks in the shade or indoors when working or exercising in the heat.
