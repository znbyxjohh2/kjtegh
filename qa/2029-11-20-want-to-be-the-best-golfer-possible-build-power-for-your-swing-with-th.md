# Want to Be the Best Golfer Possible? Build Power for Your Swing With This Full-Body Workout Plan.

This piece is part of a series of stories on the Gen Z-ificiation of golf and how the sport has evolved. Read all of the stories here.

GOLF IS EVOLVING. And so is the way guys train to hit the range and the green. A decade ago, simply hitting the links was enough of a workout. But these days, if guys want to have an edge on their buddies (and feel better about their bodies), they hit the gym, too.Pro golf is also getting younger and stronger: the median age of players at the 2024 PGA Tour Net Championships was just 31. Athletes like Bryson DeChambeau and Will Zalatoris are examples of how golfers are increasingly treating strength training as a non-negotiable tool for generating power, improving mobility, and staying injury-free.

Finding a balance between strength training and your long game is important. Playing more golf is the only way to truly improve your game, but your strength training regimen will be the X-factor that builds consistency, control, distance, and longevity.

“If the golf swing is the most dynamic and powerful movement you do on a weekly basis, you will get injured—period,” says Milo Bryant, C.S.C.S., MH contributing editor, owner of Limitless Fitness in Del Mar, California, and Titleist Performance Institute-certified coach. “You don’t have to be able to squat a truck, but... can you control and handle your own body weight? And can you control and handle that in a dynamic fashion?”

What follows is a four-week full-body program designed by Bryant to fortify the key physical qualities that make your swing more efficient and effective, with three workouts per week. Strength training is just one piece of a complex puzzle. But the best pros do it for a reason: it makes you better. This program won’t magically shave five strokes off your handicap. You will, however, be stronger, more stable, and more mobile to play the game you love.

Why You Need to Train for Golf

YOUR SWING IS everything in golf, and that’s especially true if you’re a beginner. A $1,500 set of Mizuno irons won’t do much for your game if you can’t consistently get the ball from the tee box to the fairway (or at least close). Invest in your body first.

Every aspect of this training program targets a physical trait vital to swinging a golf club. You need to be sturdy, be able to move without restriction, have strength, and be coordinated. If you lack in any of these areas, your mechanics (and scorecard) will suffer.

This isn’t your typical strength training routine. It’s void of traditional compound exercises like the bench press and barbell back squat. Instead, each session blends a dynamic warm-up, mobility drills, and mostly bodyweight and unilateral strength movements designed to improve the qualities that translate directly to your swing.

You might be tempted to just jump straight into the workouts, but this workout is designed with purpose. Do not skip your warm-up and mobility work. These exercises are essential to unrestricting your movement for sharper mechanics and better club control. You’ll train just three days a week, so your goal is simple: execute every rep with focus and intensity.

What You Need For a Better Golf Swing

Stability

“Golfers need to have a stable base on which they can move their body,” says Bryant, who considers one muscle the primary stabilizer in your swing. “The glutes are going to be huge in this—they need to be strong, they need to be active.”

Why? Your glutes stabilize and drive hip rotation throughout each phase of your swing (the backswing, transition, and downswing). When they’re weak or inactive, other muscles, like your lower back, can take over, leading to an inconsistent swing at best and an injury at worst.

Mobility

“Golf is a rotary sport, so you are going to be putting a tremendous amount of torque on your body while you’re rotating into the backswing, especially into that downswing and in that transition,” explains Bryant. “If someone can’t rotate properly, they’re going to compensate for that.”

The two areas where you need the most mobility are your thoracic spine (T-spine) and your hips. If either area is stiff, you’ll struggle to maintain posture and control throughout your swing. That’s why this program includes dynamic mobility drills to open these areas and improve your rotational range.

Strength

You don’t need powerlifter strength to crush drives, but if you’ve never lifted—or only train sporadically—building strength means generating more force to help send the ball farther down the fairway.

“There’s a direct correlation between how high you can jump and how far you can hit a ball,” explains Bryant. Exercises that make you stronger and faster will create more clubhead speed without adding bulk.

Balance

“We look at power from the lower body, core, and upper body,” says Bryant. “If they’re balanced, your body is essentially in equilibrium and has the best chance of hitting the longest distance.”

This program heavily relies on unilateral exercises, movements performed on one side at a time, to challenge balance and coordination. The ability to balance your body and perform each phase of your swing in a fluid motion creates a more repeatable swing and improves your ability to strike the ball consistently.

What Else You Should Do On This Plan

LOOK AT ANY PGA Tour roster and you’ll see plenty of elite golfers who don’t look like elite athletes. Still, building a few healthier habits into your daily routine can boost your stamina, lean you out, and help you recover faster between rounds and workouts. Below are three simple behaviors that deliver the biggest return on your efforts.

Walk 8,000-10,000 Steps Per Day

[Workout Plan](https://huanle3.com/iye1sb3o/)

Even if you ride in a cart, you’ll rack up a lot of steps during a round. Adding more walking to your non-golf days is solid sport-specific conditioning. In terms of improving your general health, any number of steps more than you’re taking now is beneficial. But we suggest aiming for between 8,000 to 10,000 daily steps, ideally closer to the top-end of that range. If you’re under that, gradually add 1,000 steps to your weekly average until you reach the goal.

Eat More Protein

Whether you want to gain, lose, or maintain your bodyweight, or maybe you don’t care much at all about the scale, try to eat more protein (if you aren’t already). Try for 20 to 30 percent of your daily calories from protein, with at least 30 grams per meal. This supports muscle repair from training, helps keep you lean, and fuels better performance.

Don’t Stop Golfing

This program is designed to complement your golf game, not replace it. Plan to take on one round and go to the range a couple of times a week. Your initial muscle soreness should subside once you get used to the workouts. Since you’re not lifting heavy weights, your overall fatigue should stay low so you can train hard without impacting your game.

HOW TO DO IT:

Sit on the ground with your legs extended in front of you, keeping them bent at 90 degrees and your weight on your heels.

Place your hands behind you, maintaining a 90-degree bend in your hip, to create more stability.

Half-Kneeling Windmill

Sets and Reps: 2 sets of 5 reps (each leg)

Get into a half-kneeling stance holding a kettlebell, with your legs positioned wider than you would for other exercises.

Raise or clean the kettlebell to your shoulder, then press it overhead. Avoid arching your back; work on keeping your ribcage nice and tight. Once overhead, make sure to keep your eyes focused on the kettlebell throughout the movement.

Slightly push your glutes back; as you’re doing this, your chest will begin rotating to that side. Squeeze your shoulder blades tight, and lower your opposite elbow to the ground. If reaching your elbow is too difficult, try just touching your hand to the ground instead.

Power out of the bottom position to return to the start. Squeeze your glutes at the top.

Reverse Lunge

Sets and Reps: 3 sets of 15 reps (each side)

Stand with your feet shoulder-width apart. Squeeze your glutes and shoulder blades, keeping your gaze neutral at a point straight ahead of you.

You can perform lunges with only your bodyweight, or use a wide range of implements like dumbbells, kettlebells, or barbells held in a number of positions. For simplicity's sake, start by holding a pair of dumbbells, one in each hand at hip-level.

Step one leg back and slightly out, landing with your toe first. Work to avoid slamming your knee into the ground. Keep your chest in an upright position, bending your knees to form right angles with both of your legs.

Drive off the ground with your front foot and step your rear leg forward into the starting position. Keep your torso in a solid upright position by squeezing your core to stay balanced.

Squat Jump

Sets and reps: 3 sets of 8 reps

Start in an athletic stance, with your feet roughly shoulder-width apart.

Shift your arms behind your body, push your butt back, bend your knees slightly. Explode up off the ground, driving your arms up over your head.

Land softly on both feet. Bend your knees to initiate the next rep, but don't rush.

Kettlebell Rainbow

Sets and Reps: 2 sets of 5 reps (on each leg)

Standing with your feet underneath your hips, holding a light kettlebell by the handle in your left hand. Your hands should be set at your shoulders, like you’re about to do a dumbbell press, and your left foot should be off the floor.

Press the kettlebell up and out so your arms are making arching motion (like a rainbow).

At the top of the movement, transfer the kettlebell to your right hand and lower both arms back to the starting position.

Complete five transfers, and then lift your other foot off the ground before doing five more reps.

Quadruped Walkthrough

Sets and Reps: 2 sets of 10 reps (each side)

Start on all fours with hands under shoulders and knees under hips.

Lift your knees an inch off the floor, keeping the core tight.

Step your right leg under your body, extending it out to the left side, until your torso is completely facing the opposite direction.

Return to the starting position and alternate sides.

Pushup

Sets and Reps: 3 sets of 20 reps

Start in a pushup position, with your palms flat on the floor, just wider than your shoulders.

Squeeze your shoulders, glutes, and core to create full-body tension. Your spine should form a straight line, with a neutral spine.

Bend your elbows to descend to the floor, stopping with your chest just above the ground. Your elbows should be at a 45 degree angle relative to the torso.

Press back up off the floor, raising up to the top position with your elbows fully extended.

Single-Arm Bent-Over Row

Sets and Reps: 3 sets of 10 reps (each arm)

Stand with your feet about hip-width apart, squeezing your shoulder blades, abs, and glutes to create tension. Grab your dumbbell with a neutral grip, then push your butt back and hinge at the hips to bend over, keeping your back flat and allowing your arm to hang down. Keep your head in a neutral position, looking down at the ground.

Move at the elbow and shoulder to row the weight to the top of your ribcage. Squeeze your obliques to keep non-working side stable.

Control the weight back down to the starting position, maintaining your posture.

Bodyweight Squat

Stand with your feet just wider than shoulder-width apart, with your toes pointed slightly out to the side. Squeeze your shoulder blades, abs, and glutes to create full-body tension. Keep your gaze straight ahead to maintain a neutral spine.

Push your butt back slightly to begin the movement, then bend at the knees and lower yourself down, opening up your knees. Keep your torso upright and maintain core tension and extend your arms out in front of yourself for balance.

Lower down until your butt is below your knees, keeping your weight in your heels. Ideal depth with vary from person to person, but make sure that you don't lose your good posture.

Stand up, squeezing your glutes to extend your hips.

Skater Lunge

Sets and Reps: 3 sets of 10 reps (each side)

Stand in an athletic stance with your feet close together, squeezing your shoulder blades, abs, and glutes.

Shift your balance to your right foot. Then explosively leap off that foot, jumping to the left.

Land lightly on your left foot. Reset, then jump back to the other side.

Turkish Situp

Sets and Reps: 3 sets of 10 reps each side

Start on the ground, lying down with a dumbbell in your left hand in a neutral grip. Extend your right arm out to the side with your palm flat on the floor. Press the weight up directly above your chest. Your left knee should be bent with your left foot flat on the floor; your right leg should be slightly out to the side from your torso. Keep your gaze fixed on the dumbbell.

Drive your shoulders up off the ground using your core, so your weight is on your right elbow. Keep the dumbbell extended overhead.

Reverse the movement step-by-step.

Inverted Row

Sets and Reps: 3 sets of 10 reps

Start with a bar placed in a rack or Smith machine, lying on your back underneath. Adjust the bar to a height just above your reach with your arms extended.

Reach up and grab the bar with an overhand grip, with your hands just wider than shoulder-width apart. Put your feet together, forming a straight line from your feet to your shoulders. Pull yourself up off the floor, squeezing your shoulder blades, abs, and glutes to create full body tension.

Pull yourself up, imagining that you're pulling the bar down to your chest. Pause for a count at the top of the movement.

Lower yourself back down under control.

Bench Pushup

Place your hands on an elevated surface (like a weight bench, plyo box, or chair) about shoulder-width apart and your feet at a slightly narrower distance. Create core tension; your abs, rib cage and glutes.

Widen your hands slightly—your shoulders should be aligned directly above your hands. Focus on keeping your shoulders directly above your hands (your arms and torso should not form a 90-degree angle at the top of the movement).

Bend at the elbows to lower your chest down just above the platform and pause to feel the contraction.

Drive back up to the start.

Med Ball Slam

Stand with your feet a little wider than shoulder-width apart, knees bent, a med ball held in both hands.

Explosively raise up onto your tiptoes, lifting the med ball high and throwing your arms over your shoulders. (You should be in triple-extension now.)

Now explosively bend your knees and hips and throw the ball straight at the ground.

Catch it on the bounce, reset, and begin another rep.

Med Ball Chest Thrust

Stand holding a medicine ball at your chest. Ideally, you do this a few yards from a wall; you can also do this while standing in an open area.

Take a forceful step forward and push the med ball away from your body as hard as you can.

See how far the med ball travels. Go grab the ball, then begin another rep. Alternate the leg you step forward with on every single rep.

Med Ball Rotary Thrust

Sets and Reps: 3 sets of 10 reps (5 each side per set)

Stand holding a medball at your chest, knees bent, feet slightly staggered, your right shoulder facing a wall.

Rotate forcefully to the left, then throw the ball to your right as hard as possible.

Pick up the ball, return to the start, and repeat. That's 1 rep.

Tennis Ball/Racquetball Throw

Sets and Reps: 2 sets of 10 reps (each arm per set)

Set up in your golf swing stance, a tennis ball held gently in both hands.

Arc upwards, as if swinging your club; as you reach the apex of your backswing, gently toss the ball upwards and catch it. (If you've struggled to keep your hips level, you may throw the ball backwards instead of directly upwards; that's the challenge of the drill).

Follow through back to the start of your golf swing. That's 1 rep.

25-Yard Progression Sprints

Sets and Reps: 10 sprints

Find a 25-yard span. Stand at the start of the span, knees bent, one foot staggered behind the other (start in the stance most comfortable to you).

Lean your chest forwards, then explosively sprint forward until you've reached the end of you 25-yard span. That's 1 rep.

Rest 30-60 seconds between each rep (if you need slightly more rest, that's OK too).
