Can ADHD medications cause movement disorders in children? Yes, ADHD medications can affect movement in children, sometimes triggering movement disorders such as tics, tremors, or dyskinesias, and altering natural body rhythms. Stimulants like methylphenidate and amphetamines are most commonly linked to these effects, though non-stimulants may also contribute. Most movement-related side effects are mild and reversible, but persistent or severe symptoms should be evaluated by a healthcare provider.

Explanation

ADHD medications, particularly stimulants like methylphenidate, can impact how children move. According to the first reference, these medications can sometimes cause movement disorders. This means that a child might experience unusual movements or changes in how they move.

The second reference explains that methylphenidate can also change the body's natural rhythms, which are like internal clocks that help regulate sleep and daily activities. These changes could affect how often or how a child moves, especially if their body's rhythm is altered.
