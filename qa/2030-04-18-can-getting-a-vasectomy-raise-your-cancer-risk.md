# ​Can Getting a Vasectomy Raise Your Cancer Risk?

VASECTOMIES HAVE BEEN around for a long time, but they're having a bigger moment now than they used to. Research presented at the American Urological Association's annual meeting in 2024 says rates have risen since the reversal of Roe v Wade in 2022.

Vasectomies—a surgical procedure that prevents the release of sperm—are a very effective method of birth control. But research has gone back and forth about what they might do to a man's risk of prostate cancer.

A large study in the Journal of Clinical Oncology in 2017 added more support in favor of the procedure’s safety. After studying over 84,000 men for 15 years, researchers discovered there was no elevated risk for overall, advanced-stage prostate cancer, or death due to prostate cancer after undergoing a vasectomy.

The authors wrote in study that “there is no established biologic rationale for an association of vasectomy with prostate cancer.” With a vasectomy, the vans deferens is cut, blocked, or sealed to prevent the release of sperm. Previously, researchers investigated whether that could cause an immunological response or changes in cell division that could lead to cancer, but those biological mechanisms in humans have remained unclear and unsubstantiated.

A much newer study, published in 2024 in the journal Prostate, controlled for BMI and PSA testing and also didn't support vasectomies as a risk factor for prostate cancer.

Four of seven previous studies prior to 2017 looking at vasectomy and prostate cancer found no link either, MedPage Today reports, though one did find an overall increased risk, as well as an increase in high-risk tumors, too.

Bottom line: If you’re looking for a permanent form of birth control, don’t let fears of prostate cancer cloud your decision. In an accompanying editorial of the new study, researchers from Harvard concluded that though there is some conflicting evidence, they “believe it should carry little weight in the context of other considerations relevant to deciding whether to have a vasectomy.”
