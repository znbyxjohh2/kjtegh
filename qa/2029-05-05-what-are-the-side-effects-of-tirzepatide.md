Mechanism of Action

Tirzepatide works as a mimetic meaning it mimics naturally occurring hormones and increases the responses caused by said hormones. Tirzepatide is considered an incretin mimetic as it replicates the effects regulated by incretin hormones. The molecule is a synthetic peptide consisting of 39 amino acids which is currently the first and only compound of its kind to be a dual-targeting drug.

Tirzepatide’s structure gives it both glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) and glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) properties, meaning its unique structure gives it a larger range of biochemical behavior than a single-acting incretin mimetic. Biologically, GLP-1 and GIP interact in pathways responsible for preserving a normal glycemic state, appetite and satiety responses as well as carbohydrate and lipid metabolism. Tirzepatide replicates this response by activating GLP-1 and GIP receptors to induce these responses.

Moreover, tirzepatide has a stronger affinity to GIP receptors, longer bioavailability than our naturally occurring incretins, and synergistic effects due to it being a dual GLP-1/GIP agonist.

Precautions

Before starting tirzepatide it is important to discuss and evaluate with a doctor the risks and possible outcomes of using the medication. Research has found hypersensitivity to GLP-1 and GIL receptor agonists that can cause anaphylaxis and angioedema. Considering that the active ingredient, tirzepatide, biochemically behaves as a dual agonist; you should disclose to your doctor if you have any known allergies to any ingredients in Mounjaro or Zepbound, including GLP-1 and GIL receptor agonists.

If use of either drug results in rashes, itching, difficulty breathing, or any other severe allergy-related symptoms you need to talk to a healthcare provider immediately. Additionally, individuals who are pregnant or plan to become pregnant should not consider tirzepatide as studies have identified possible harm to fetus health during pregnancy. Moreover, sincere caution should be taken if you’re breastfeeding considering the research around tirzepatide and adverse pregnancy outcomes. Tirzepatide is not prescribed to treat type 1 diabetes, nor is it prescribed in individuals at risk for thyroid or adrenal tumors.

Potential Interactions

It is important that your doctor knows any and all medications, vitamins, or other supplements you take or have taken in the past in order to have a comprehensive understanding of what interactions may take place. One of the most common drug interactions is the combined effects of insulin and glycemic regulators when used with GLP-1/GIL agonists. The possible hypoglycemic effect of using more than one drug with blood-glucose lowering properties should be closely monitored.

Tirzepatide may have synergistic effects when taken with other medications, such as antidiabetic drugs, but it can also have adverse effects. For example, tirzepatide should not be taken with any other GLP-1 or GIL agonists such as semaglutide, as the drugs are contraindicated. Additionally, tirzepatide use can decrease the efficacy of other medications like birth control. Thus your doctor may want to switch or alter other prescriptions you’re currently taking. Moreover, do not start any over the counter (OTC) medications without consulting a healthcare provider first, especially painkillers, cold or flu meds, and allergy medications.
