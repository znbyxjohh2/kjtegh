# When Do You Need a Colonoscopy?

It’s an uncomfortable test with lifesaving results: A recent study in the New England Journal of Medicine found that colonoscopies can reduce your risk of death by colon cancer by more than 50 percent. Here’s the quick and dirty on when you should be tested and what symptoms warrant a call to your doc.

If you're older than 50:“If you’re 50 years old, no matter what, you should have colonoscopy done,” says Anthony Kalloo, M.D., Director of Gastroenterology and Hepatology at The Johns Hopkins Hospital. The procedure takes a long, flexible tube and inserts it through your rectum. That helps doctors look for polyps—precancerous formations—that, if removed, can stop cancer in its tracks. Schedule it with any gastroenterologist.

If you're African American and 45 or older:“If you’re African American, you should get screened at 45,” says Kalloo. A recent study published in JAMA found that African American males had an increased risk of colon cancer a younger age than white males.

If you have a family history of colon cancer:If a first-degree relative had colon cancer, then your risk is much higher than those with no family history. Generally, if you have a family history, the recommended age for screening is 45. However, if you know the age the person in your family was when they got diagnosed, you should get scanned 10 years before that age. So if dad got diagnosed at 47, you need to be screened at 37.

If you don't work out regularly:Working out stimulates your gastrointestinal tract, which regulates the digestion process and keeps your colon actively working. “A longer exposure to your stool’s toxins in your colon means longer exposure to waste that’s sitting there,” says Mark Welton, M.D., a Men's Health advisor and chief of colorectal surgery at the Stanford Cancer Center. If you have a diet high in red meat, and fat, or low in fiber:A diet high in fiber bulks up your stool, which keeps waste moving. expose your body to those toxins, Welton adds. In countries like Africa, where people’s diets are high in fiber and low in fat, fewer incidents of colon cancer are reported. “We attribute this to their diets because western culture has this high fat, low fiber diet,” Welton adds.
