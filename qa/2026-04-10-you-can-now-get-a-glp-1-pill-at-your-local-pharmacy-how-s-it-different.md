How Effective Is the Wegovy Pill?

GLP-1s, OR GLUCAGON-LIKE peptide-1 receptor agonists, are used to treat type 2 diabetes and obesity by mimicking your body’s natural GLP-1 hormone. This helps people manage blood sugar, suppress appetite, and feel full.

The Wegovy pill has been found to be just as effective as the injectable for weight loss, says Jean Park, M.D., an endocrinologist at MedStar Health in Baltimore, Maryland.

People who took 25 mg of semaglutide (the type of GLP-1 that Wegovy contains) orally every day for 64 weeks experienced a 13.6 percent drop in body weight, according to a 2025 study published in The New England Journal of Medicine and funded by Novo Nordisk (Wegovy’s manufacturer).

Novo Nordisk reported that, in a separate trial, people lost 16.6 percent of their body weight after taking the pill.

By comparison, in another Novo Nordisk-funded study published in 2021, people who injected 2.4 mg of semaglutide once a week and made lifestyle changes had a 14.9 percent lower body weight after 68 weeks.

“This is quite clinically meaningful for improving metabolic health,” Dr. Gendreau says.

But just like with the injectable, the GLP-1 pill works best when paired with strength training, increased protein intake, good sleep, and adequate hydration, he says. “Medication can open the door, but lifestyle still drives long-term health.”

THE PILL AND injectable forms of Wegovy have similar side effects—most commonly, nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea, Dr. Park says. Acid reflux, constipation, and abdominal cramps are also common, Dr. Gendreau says.

“In my weight loss clinic, we emphasize eating slowly, stopping eating at the first sign of fullness,” he says, as well as avoiding greasy, fried, and processed foods and sugar, increasing protein and fiber, and drinking eight to 10 cups of water per day.

How Often Do You Take the Pill?

THE WEGOVY PILL should be taken once a day. But when you take it matters.

It “must be taken once daily on an empty stomach with up to 4 ounces of plain water, at least 30 minutes before the first food and drink or other oral medications of the day,” Dr. Park says. “This is so the medication can be absorbed effectively.”

This is one difference between the pill and injectable, Dr. Gendreau says. “The once-weekly injectable doesn’t have this type of timing stipulation.”
