# Q: How Do I Beat Stubborn Acne?

This week’s question is a tough one. This reader obviously read and followed all of our suggestions about eliminating acne (including our Questions of the Week), but nothing seemed to work. So we decided to ask Manhattan dermatologist Dr. Neal Schultz for his expert input.

[Do Beat Stubborn](https://metabolic-horizon.bbsxyxy.com/)

I'm a 28-year-old guy who just developed bad skin about 7 months ago. I currently use an oil-free acne wash twice a day except when I substitute an apricot exfoliating scrub 3-4 times per week. I also apply a calming lotion every morning. I wash my face twice a day, use a new towel every time, don't touch my face during the day, clean my phone with alcohol, don't eat many sugary foods and don't have much dairy. The acne is mainly on my cheeks. My face is very dry after washing so I'm concerned that this is the cause. How can I keep from having the skin of a kid in junior high? Any recommendations you have would be most appreciated. Please help!

Follow @MensHealthStyle on Twitter for more answers to your style and grooming questions.

The areas affected by your new acne are unusual in that they’re on your cheeks instead of in the t-zone. For non-inflammatory white heads and black heads, spot treat with products with 1 or 2 percent salicylic acid, or use 5 to 8 percent glycolic gels and solutions. For inflammatory acne (red pimples, pus pimples, cysts), use products with benzyl peroxide, sulfa or resorcinol once or twice a day, after cleansing and before moisturizing. Because cheeks have fewer oil glands, they're less prone to acne breakouts and more prone to over-drying. Unfortunately both are recurring there for you at this time. The granular scrub is probably too strong and further aggravating the dryness. Use Neutrogena Oil Free Acne Wash twice a day. After washing with it, pat dry and apply appropriate spot treatments to the breakouts and then apply an oil-free water based moisturizer to the dry areas.

While the calming lotion is usually for sensitive irritated skin, it might be complicating the issue. For a gentle cleanser, consider a Cetaphil product. You can use that on one side and the Neutrogena on the other side and see if there’s a difference in dryness.

It’s not necessary to use a new towel every time you wash, although it is helpful that you don’t touch your face during the day. Diet probably has very little impact on these types of breakouts. Most shaving creams don’t have much impact because their contact time is so brief, but using a shaving foam or gel is less apt to cause clogs than shaving cream.

Good luck!
