PRP for Hair Regrowth: Does it Actually Work for Thinning?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

PRP therapy uses a concentration of your own platelets, rich in growth factors, to stimulate hair follicles. Clinical trials show it can increase hair density and thickness in mild to moderate pattern hair loss. However, results vary based on your thinning stage, overall health, and your provider's skill and protocol.

Key factors to weigh include candidacy, treatment steps, timeline, risks, costs, and alternative therapies—all detailed below to guide your next steps.

Because hair loss can stem from many causes—hormonal shifts, nutritional deficiencies, autoimmune conditions, or stress—PRP isn't the right answer for everyone. Explanation

Hair thinning affects millions of people worldwide and can take a toll on self-esteem. Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) has emerged as a popular, minimally invasive option for improving hair density. But does PRP for hair loss really work? Below, we break down the science, the procedure, the pros and cons, and what you need to know before trying it yourself.

What Is PRP?

PRP stands for platelet-rich plasma, a concentration of platelets derived from your own blood. Platelets are rich in growth factors and cytokines—messenger proteins that support tissue repair and regeneration.

Key steps in creating PRP:

A small blood sample (20–60 mL) is drawn from your arm.

Blood is spun in a centrifuge to separate red cells, white cells, plasma, and platelets.

The plasma layer, now laden with platelets, is collected for injection into the scalp.

How PRP Is Thought to Work

When injected into areas of thinning scalp, PRP's growth factors may:

Stimulate hair follicle stem cells

Extend the anagen (growth) phase of the hair cycle

Promote new blood vessel formation (angiogenesis)

Reduce inflammation around follicles

These effects can lead to thicker, stronger hair over time. However, individual responses vary based on genetics, underlying conditions, and treatment technique.

What Does the Research Say?

Multiple studies and reviews support PRP's potential for hair regrowth, especially in androgenetic alopecia (pattern hair loss):

• Meta-analysis in the Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology (2020) – Included 11 clinical trials, 308 patients – Reported significant increases in hair density (average gain: 37 hairs/cm²) – Noted improvements in hair thickness and patient satisfaction

• Trial in Dermatologic Surgery (2019) – Randomized, split-scalp design – Showed PRP-treated areas had 30% more hair count versus control

• Guidelines from the American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) – Acknowledges PRP as a promising option for mild to moderate androgenetic alopecia – Advises more large-scale studies to standardize protocols

While evidence is encouraging, PRP is not a guaranteed "cure." Results depend on your hair loss stage, overall health, and the skill of the provider.

Who Is a Good Candidate?

PRP for hair loss may be suitable if you:

Have early to moderate androgenetic alopecia (male or female pattern baldness)

Are in good general health

Have realistic expectations (improvement, not full restoration)

Are not pregnant or severely anemic

Do not have a blood-clotting disorder or active scalp infection

PRP tends to be less effective for advanced baldness (Norwood scale V–VII in men) or scarring alopecias. If your hair loss is sudden, patchy, or accompanied by itching or redness, it's important to identify the underlying cause before committing to any treatment—a free symptom checker can help you understand what might be causing your symptoms and whether you should seek immediate medical attention.

What to Expect During the PRP Procedure

Consultation– Medical history review, scalp exam, blood tests if needed

Blood Draw– Similar to a standard lab draw (20–60 mL)

Centrifugation– Blood spins for 10–15 minutes to concentrate platelets

Scalp Preparation– Cleansing and optional topical numbing to reduce discomfort

Injection– Multiple small injections across the thinning areas– Procedure time: 30–60 minutes

Most people tolerate the injections well, reporting only mild discomfort. You can resume normal activities immediately, though providers often advise avoiding strenuous exercise for 24 hours.

Results and Timeline

PRP is not a one-and-done treatment. Typical protocol:

3 sessions spaced 4–6 weeks apart

Maintenance sessions every 4–6 months

When to expect visible changes:

Risks and Side Effects

PRP is generally safe since it uses your own blood. Possible side effects include:

Temporary soreness, redness, or bruising at injection sites

Mild headache

Rare infection (minimized by sterile technique)

Serious complications are extremely rare. Always choose a qualified provider who follows strict hygiene protocols.

Cost Considerations

PRP for hair loss is considered cosmetic and is not covered by most insurance plans. Typical costs:

$500–$2,000 per session, depending on location and provider expertise

Total initial series (3 sessions): $1,500–$6,000

Maintenance: $500–$1,500 per session

Compare pricing, credentials, and before-and-after photos when selecting a clinic.

Alternatives to PRP

If PRP isn't right for you—or if you want to combine approaches—consider:

Topical minoxidil (Rogaine®): FDA-approved, over-the-counter

Oral finasteride (Propecia®): Prescription pill for men

Low-level laser therapy (LLLT): At-home devices or in-office

Hair transplant surgery: Follicular unit extraction (FUE) or strip harvest

Nutritional support: Biotin, iron, vitamin D if deficiencies are present

A dermatologist or hair restoration specialist can help you tailor a multi-modal plan.

Setting Realistic Expectations

PRP can boost hair thickness and slow shedding, but it won't create a full head of hair where follicles no longer exist. Treatment success depends on:

Your degree of thinning

Underlying health and nutrition

Consistency with sessions and maintenance therapies

Avoid providers promising "complete regrowth" or "permanent cure." Instead, look for measured claims backed by clinical data.

Next Steps

If you're curious about the cause of your thinning or suspect an autoimmune component, understanding your specific type of hair loss is crucial. Speak to a doctor before starting PRP or any treatment that could interact with medications or health conditions. Only a qualified medical professional can determine the safest, most effective plan for your unique situation.
