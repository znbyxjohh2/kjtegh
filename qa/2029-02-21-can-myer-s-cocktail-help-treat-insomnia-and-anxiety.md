The role of nutritional deficiencies in insomnia and anxiety

Magnesium is a mineral that has a calming effect on the brain. A magnesium deficiency can leave individuals feeling fatigued, panicked, and stressed.

Magnesium deficiency can have a snowball effect and can make you more susceptible to high anxiety and stress levels. Elevated stress levels can increase magnesium loss through urination, and alcohol and caffeine compound this effect. The more magnesium you lose, the more stress and anxiety you may feel.

A deficiency in vitamin B6 (pyridoxine) typically manifests itself in confusion, disturbed sleep, emotional disturbances, and irritability, among other adverse effects. Furthermore, vitamin B6 is essential for the synthesis of dopamine and tryptophan.

Tryptophan is an essential amino acid that regulates sleep and mood. Not getting enough vitamin B6 from your diet can disturb your body’s metabolism of tryptophan, limiting the amount of serotonin in your body and potentially causing sleep disturbances and insomnia.

Research on Myer’s Cocktail for insomnia and anxiety

While there is limited research on using Myer’s Cocktail specifically for insomnia and anxiety, magnesium and B vitamin supplementation may help reduce symptoms and improve sleep quality in individuals with these conditions.

In one study on older people with insomnia, taking 500 milligrams (mg) of magnesium daily for eight weeks improved both subjective measures of insomnia (such as early morning awakening, ISI score, sleep efficiency, sleep onset latency, and sleep time) and objective measures of insomnia (such as concentration of melatonin, serum cortisol, and serum renin).1

A systematic review of several studies found magnesium supplementation can be beneficial in reducing mild anxiety and premenstrual symptoms.

In a study of 478 young adults, vitamin B6 supplementation reduced self-reported anxiety and depression, and vitamin B12 supplementation caused changes in stress.
