Why do I experience chest pain while sleeping on my left side?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Lying on your left side can cause chest pain by shifting the position of your heart, lungs, and stomach. This posture may trigger acid reflux, place pressure on the chest wall, or aggravate underlying conditions—most causes are noncardiac, but symptoms should never be ignored.

Chest pain has many possible causes, from harmless muscle strain to serious cardiac concerns, and pinpointing the source on your own is difficult. Taking a free, instant, online symptom check can help you understand what may be behind your discomfort and guide your next steps—whether that's self-care, seeing a doctor, or seeking urgent care.

Explanation

When you sleep on your left side, your body's position can affect how your chest feels. One possibility is that the pressure from your weight on that side might press slightly on your heart or the tissues near it. For some people, this added pressure can trigger discomfort even if it's not a serious heart condition.

Another reason could be acid reflux. When you sleep on your left side, stomach acid may travel up into the esophagus (the tube that connects your mouth to your stomach), causing irritation and chest pain. In addition, musculoskeletal issues such as inflammation of the cartilage in your chest (costochondritis) could be affected by this sleeping position.
