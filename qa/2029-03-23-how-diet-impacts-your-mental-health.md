Healthy eating versus disordered patterns

For some people, distinguishing between maintaining a healthy diet and becoming overly fixated on their eating habits can be challenging. What you eat can significantly impact your mental health — both positively and negatively. While incorporating more healthy fats, protein, fruits, and vegetables in daily meals can help elevate your mood and enhance your overall mental well-being, it’s crucial to remember that your diet should not prevent you from enjoying life. Without balance, a fixation on dieting can potentially become harmful and lead to an eating disorder.1

Eating disorders are mental health disorders that affect approximately 5% of the population and can have severe consequences for a person's physical and mental well-being.21 There are various types of eating disorders, with the most common ones being anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, and binge-eating disorder. These disorders come with physical, mental, and emotional symptoms. Some general mental health symptoms associated with eating disorders include:2

Binge-eating disorder

Binge-eating disorder (BED) is characterized by consuming a large amount of food within a short period. During a binge episode, individuals may continue eating even when they are already full. Often, they are unaware of the amount they are consuming and experience intense guilt afterward.5 A couple of the physical conditions associated with binge-eating disorder are type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease.5

Eating disorders are serious mental health conditions that require prompt attention and treatment. Fortunately, there are various avenues through which individuals struggling with an eating disorder can access the help they need. If you or someone you know may be suffering from an eating disorder, consider taking the following steps:

Reach out to a trusted person for support

Attend group therapy sessions

Find a therapist specializing in eating disorder treatment

Consider an in-patient residential program

Sharing meals

Individuals grappling with an eating disorder often isolate themselves and spend significant time concealing their condition from loved ones. This is why sharing a meal with a friend or family member can be seen as a significant step on the road to recovery.

But, even for individuals not dealing with an eating disorder, eating meals with others offers numerous psychological and social benefits. Research has demonstrated that communal eating can directly influence your overall mood. The more time you spend eating with others, the happier and more satisfied you tend to be with life due to an increased sense of belonging and community.6

Healthy eating and mental health

Different foods provide the body with various vitamins and minerals, underscoring the importance of dietary variety. Food can influence your mood by affecting serotonin levels, a neurotransmitter that regulates sleep, mood, and appetite. Since most of your body’s serotonin is produced in the gut, what you eat plays a vital role in determining the amount of serotonin in your system.22

Including whole grains, fruits, and vegetables in your daily diet can significantly impact your physical and mental well-being. These food groups contain naturally occurring serotonin and dopamine, which can enhance overall mood. They also provide essential vitamins and minerals for your body's optimal functioning. Foods like fish, fresh vegetables, and fruits are rich in mood-boosting nutrients and can potentially alleviate symptoms of anxiety and depression.7

Protein and amino acids

Protein, composed of amino acids, offers numerous benefits to your body, including the breakdown of food, growth, and repair of body tissues, energy provision, maintenance of healthy skin and nails, muscle development, immune system support, normal digestion, and the production of various hormones and neurotransmitters.

To ensure your body functions properly, you need 20 different types of amino acids. Different foods contain different amino acids, so it’s crucial to incorporate a wide range of healthy foods into your diet to ensure you get the necessary nutrients.

Complete proteins are foods that contain all nine essential amino acids. Some examples include beef, poultry, fish, eggs, dairy products, soy, quinoa, and buckwheat.

Incomplete proteins contain some, but not all, of the necessary amino acids. Nuts, seeds, beans, and certain grains fall into this category.8

Healthy fats

Healthy fats are essential for supporting proper brain function. Your brain consists of approximately 60% fat, highlighting the importance of incorporating the right types of fats into your diet.23 Omega-3 fatty acids, in particular, play a crucial role in learning and memory. You can find omega-3s in foods like salmon, sardines, and other fatty fish.9

Eating regularly

Your brain is constantly active, even while you sleep, and it relies on a steady supply of fuel to function optimally. This fuel typically comes from the nutrients obtained through your meals. Consistently eating at regular intervals throughout the day ensures a reliable energy source for your brain. Regular meals also prevent significant fluctuations in blood sugar levels, which can negatively impact mood and lead to fatigue or irritability.10

The importance of hydration

Water makes up a significant portion of your body weight, ranging from 50% to 70%. Adequate hydration is crucial, as it aids in waste elimination, moderates body temperature, lubricates and cushions joints, and protects sensitive tissues. Dehydration can leave you feeling tired, overwhelmed, and struggling to complete daily tasks, ultimately affecting your mental health and potentially leading to increased stress levels.

Your adequate daily fluid intake should be about 11.5-15.5 cups of water (about 20% of which comes from food) daily to maintain proper hydration and support your mental well-being.11

How caffeine can influence your mood

Caffeine is an addictive substance that helps people stay awake and, in theory, more focused. However, caffeine can only provide artificial energy, not the naturally occurring energy your body needs. It works by blocking adenosine, a chemical that accumulates during the day and induces feelings of tiredness at night. By binding to adenosine receptors, caffeine prevents drowsiness and allows dopamine to flow more freely, resulting in a perceived sense of energy and alertness.

Caffeine can have both positive and negative effects on mental health, depending on the amount consumed. Moderate coffee consumption, around one or two cups per day, has been associated with reduced cognitive decline, improved learning consolidation, and even a lower risk of depression. However, excessive caffeine intake can lead to increased anxiety, irritability, insomnia, rapid heartbeat, and restlessness. If you enjoy coffee and prefer not to eliminate it from your diet, it's important to be mindful of your consumption and how it affects your mood and sleep patterns.12

The gut-brain connection

The gut and the brain communicate through the vagus nerve, establishing a direct link between the two. The gut can affect emotions in the brain, while the brain can influence the types of bacteria that flourish in the gut. These two systems are intricately connected, meaning that what you eat directly impacts how your brain functions. Research has revealed that 95% of the body's natural serotonin supply, a chemical responsible for regulating mood, is produced in the gut. Consequently, your dietary choices can significantly impact how you feel.22

How stress affects digestion

Stress and digestion are closely linked. When an individual experiences stress, their digestion slows down as the body enters a fight-or-flight response. Under intense stress, the digestive system cannot function properly.13

Other ways to take care of your mental health

In addition to maintaining a balanced diet, incorporating self-care activities into your daily routine can significantly improve your mental health. Below, we detail some various self-care strategies.

Exercise regularly

It's no secret that exercise can benefit your mental health, but let's delve into how it works. When you engage in physical activity, you increase blood flow to your brain, which affects the parts of the brain responsible for your stress response. Exercise also promotes social interactions and self-efficacy while serving as a healthy distraction from everyday stressors. Studies have shown that regular exercise can reduce symptoms of depression, anxiety, and negative mood while improving self-esteem and cognitive function. So, not only does exercise keep you physically fit, but it also boosts your mood and strengthens your overall well-being.14

Practice good sleep habits

Sleep plays a vital role in maintaining your physical and mental health. When you get sufficient sleep, your body functions optimally. Quality sleep enhances focus, memory, and the ability to cope with daily challenges and changes. On the other hand, a lack of sleep can be detrimental to your mental health. Insomnia, often associated with anxiety or depression, can create a vicious cycle where inadequate sleep exacerbates these conditions, which can cause further issues sleeping.15

Reduce screen time

In a world dominated by digital devices, taking breaks from screens can profoundly impact your mental health. Reducing your screen time may help reduce stress and enhance productivity.16 Limiting screen time also opens up opportunities to spend quality time with loved ones and engage in community activities. By reducing reliance on devices, you can create more opportunities for meaningful interactions and connections with those who matter most.

Additionally, cutting back on screen time can improve your sleep (which, as we covered, is essential to your mental well-being). The blue light emitted by phones and devices can confuse your brain, leading it to believe that it's still daytime and preventing the production of melatonin, the hormone that makes you sleepy.24 To prevent this, try to refrain from using devices at least one hour before bed; this allows your brain to wind down and prepare for a restful sleep.

Consider therapy

Therapy is another powerful tool for improving your mental health. With various therapeutic approaches available (including various online options), it's easier than ever to find the one that works for you. One-on-one cognitive behavioral therapy (talk therapy) or group therapy sessions may offer different benefits depending on your preferences. Therapy can help reduce stress, address past traumas, resolve conflicts, enhance communication skills, and provide coping strategies for significant life changes.17 Through therapy, you can gain insight into how your thoughts, emotions, and behaviors impact your mood, enabling you to better cultivate a balanced lifestyle that fosters overall mental well-being.
