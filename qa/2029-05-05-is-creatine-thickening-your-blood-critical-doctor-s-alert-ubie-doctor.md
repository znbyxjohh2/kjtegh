# Is Creatine Thickening Your Blood? Critical Doctor’s Alert | Ubie Doctor's Note

Published on: 5/6/2026

Blood Thickness: Does Creatine Impact Your Red Blood Cell Count?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Does creatine increase red blood cell count or hematocrit? For most healthy adults, creatine taken at standard doses does not meaningfully raise red blood cell count or hematocrit. In fact, creatine can slightly lower hematocrit by expanding plasma volume, which helps keep blood viscosity within a normal range. Staying well hydrated, sticking to recommended dosing (typically 3–5 grams daily), and getting routine bloodwork are the best ways to monitor blood health while supplementing.

That said, individual responses vary, and certain warning signs or pre-existing conditions warrant closer attention—see details below.

Explanation

Creatine is one of the most popular supplements among athletes, gym-goers, and anyone looking to boost strength and muscle mass. But with its rise in use comes questions about safety—specifically, whether creatine affects your red blood cell count or "blood thickness." In other words, could supplementing with creatine change your hematocrit levels?

This article breaks down the science in straightforward language, reviews credible research, and offers practical tips to keep your blood health on track.

What Are Hematocrit Levels and Why They Matter

Hematocrit is the percentage of your blood volume made up by red blood cells (RBCs).

Men: roughly 40%–52%

Women: roughly 36%–48%

Higher hematocrit = thicker blood; lower hematocrit = thinner blood.

Too high: headaches, dizziness, risk of clotting

Too low: fatigue, shortness of breath, anemia

Monitoring hematocrit levels is a routine part of blood tests, especially for athletes, people with heart or kidney conditions, and those on certain medications.

How Creatine Works in Your Body

Creatine is a natural compound found in muscle cells.

It stores high-energy phosphate groups to produce ATP, the fuel your muscles use during short, intense activity.

When you supplement, you raise your muscle creatine stores by about 20%–40%.

A side effect: creatine draws water into muscle tissue, increasing intracellular water content.

Because of this water-shifting effect, some worry that:

Hematocrit levels rise (blood becomes "thicker").

Is that concern justified? Let's look at the evidence.

What Research Says About Creatine and Hematocrit Levels

Over the past two decades, several studies have explored whether creatine impacts red blood cell count or hematocrit.

No significant change in hematocrit

A randomized trial of healthy young men found no meaningful differences in hematocrit or hemoglobin after 5 g/day of creatine for 4 weeks.

Conclusion: At typical dosages, creatine does not thicken blood.

Slight plasma volume expansion

Some research suggests creatine may increase total blood volume by drawing water into muscle.

Result: A slight drop or no change in hematocrit, because plasma (liquid portion of blood) expands to balance water shifts.

Individual variability

Hydration status, training load, diet, and genetics can influence outcomes.

In dehydrated athletes, the blood-thickening risk comes from dehydration itself, not creatine.

Overall, the consensus in peer-reviewed journals is that creatine and hematocrit levels are largely unaffected in healthy individuals when you follow dosing guidelines and stay well hydrated.

Who Should Be Extra Cautious?

While creatine is generally safe, certain groups may want to check with a healthcare provider before starting:

People with chronic kidney disease or reduced kidney function

Those with pre-existing high hematocrit or polycythemia vera

Individuals on medications that affect fluid balance (e.g., diuretics)

Anyone prone to dehydration (e.g., by illness, hot climate, intense endurance sports)

If you fall into one of these categories, speak to a doctor before beginning creatine supplementation.

When to See a Doctor

Always consider professional medical advice if you experience:

Severe fatigue or shortness of breath

Chest pain or rapid heart rate

Swelling in the legs or unexplained weight gain

Dark, concentrated urine or signs of dehydration that don't improve with fluids

These symptoms could signal serious issues unrelated to creatine, and only a medical professional can properly diagnose and treat them.

Key Takeaways

Current evidence shows no consistent effect of creatine on red blood cell count or hematocrit levels in healthy adults.

Creatine's water-pulling action tends to expand plasma volume, which can actually lower hematocrit slightly.

Staying well hydrated and using standard dosages helps maintain normal blood thickness.

Regular blood tests (CBC, kidney function) are a smart way to monitor any supplement regimen.
